New Zealand.

RNZ's first campaign poll has National at its lowest point since 2020, with the Greens up sharply and Labour's lead growing despite its own slip. The result lands just before tonight's head to head debate between Luxon and Hipkins on TVNZ. National's Simeon Brown acknowledged only that the election will be tight. Separately, National has promised to cut foreign aid outside the Pacific to lift Pharmac's budget and extend free cancer screening. Winston Peters called the move desperate and dangerous, since foreign affairs sits inside his own portfolio. National has also promised eight hundred million dollars for a Christchurch Northern Motorway extension, which it says will cut travel times and reduce deaths and serious injuries by seventy eight percent.

A gas main ruptured on Khyber Pass Road in Newmarket, Auckland. Nearby buildings were evacuated as a precaution while fire crews and a hazmat unit worked to make the area safe.

The Crown has opened its case in the Loafers Lodge manslaughter trial in Wellington, alleging layers of failings in fire safety systems. Four people are on trial over the blaze.

In the Bay of Plenty, Western Bay of Plenty councillor Alan Kurtovich has confirmed he quit over the council's Māori wards decision, triggering a byelection, saying the decision was right despite the cost to his seat. Separately, a man who stabbed a friend to death after a party, Dallas Hohua, has had his manslaughter sentence reduced and may serve as little as two years.

World.

Yemeni military says it has retaken the port city of Mocha near the Bab al-Mandab Strait, at the start of a Saudi-backed offensive against the Houthis, who control much of the Red Sea coast. Shipping operators remain wary of Red Sea routes despite a Houthi pledge to end targeting of vessels.

Right-wing candidate Flávio Bolsonaro has won the first round of Brazil's presidential election. Neither candidate took more than fifty percent of the vote, so the race goes to a runoff on the twenty fifth of October.

A fifteen year old's hand was blown off during a confrontation between protesting students and police in Pas-de-Calais, France. Authorities say his life is not in danger. France is bracing for a national day of school protests following the injury and mass arrests.

The Pentagon has stopped using Anthropic's AI tools after blacklisting the company in February, labelling it a supply chain risk after Anthropic refused to remove safety guardrails from its systems.

Technology.

The Pentagon's blacklisting of Anthropic, confirmed to the BBC, has led the department to stop using its AI tools altogether. Anthropic had refused to strip safety guardrails that the department wanted removed, and the company was flagged as a supply chain risk as early as February. The dispute highlights growing friction between AI developers and government agencies over how far safety limits can be relaxed for official use.

Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey has repeated his warning that the scale of money flowing into artificial intelligence could trigger market shocks, and says regulating AI directly is not the right place to start. He wants rigorous testing and safeguards instead, with the central bank watching investment flows closely.
