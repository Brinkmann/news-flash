The Resource Management Act's replacement laws have passed in Parliament, the lead story today.

New Zealand.

Parliament has passed the Government's replacement for the Resource Management Act. Minister Chris Bishop says the old law stifled economic growth. Opposition parties say the new laws will weaken environmental protection, reported by RNZ. The bills now move to implementation, with regulations to follow. Three bills overhauling references to the Treaty of Waitangi passed their first reading under urgency over the weekend and go next to the Justice Select Committee. The Human Rights Commission says it is deeply concerned by the overhaul, reported by RNZ. Nicky Hager's new book Dirty Work accuses the Taxpayers' Union and pollster David Farrar of misconduct during the twenty twenty three election campaign. Farrar has denied some allegations and dismissed another as, in his words, hilarious. The Taxpayers' Union's Jordan Williams has responded to accusations of sexual harassment in the book, reported separately by RNZ and the Herald. Westpac is investigating an outage on its Westpac One Business platform that left businesses unable to make PAYE payments, a matter for the bank to resolve before the next payment cycle. The national tarakihi catch limit has been cut to ten fish per person, a matter of record, with fishing advocacy group LegaSea warning of possible court challenges. A stocktake of half a billion dollars of information technology projects at the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment has found deficiencies in controls, triggered by a bungled immigration initiative the ministry earlier misled MPs about, reported by RNZ.

In the Bay of Plenty, Dallas Hohua, who stabbed a friend to death after a party, has had his sentence reduced and may now serve as little as two years for manslaughter, reported by the Bay of Plenty Times. Western Bay of Plenty councillor Alan Kurtovich has confirmed he quit over the council's Māori wards decision, triggering a byelection.

World.

Typhoon Dujuan has killed four people in Japan. Nearly two million people were urged to shelter, and flights were grounded, confirmed by the BBC and Stuff. The storm has since pulled away from the country. A second UK air traffic control outage in two weeks has caused fresh flight delays, with airlines demanding action and renewed calls for the head of the air traffic organisation to resign, reported by the BBC. Separately, a cable cut by construction workers delayed flights at major United States airports before repairs restored normal operations. The United States has signed a deal with Denmark to build two military bases in Greenland, reaffirming Danish sovereignty over the territory while deepening the American security presence there, confirmed by the BBC and Al Jazeera. A Sri Lankan court has convicted fifteen men over the twenty nineteen Easter Sunday bombings that killed two hundred and seventy people at churches and hotels, a matter of record. The G7 has called on Iran to stop arming Yemen's Houthi movement, condemning Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia as a threat to global energy security, as fighting in Yemen escalates.

Technology.

King Charles has warned of what he called the existential danger of artificial intelligence falling into the wrong hands, speaking at an Ayrshire summit attended by Nvidia, OpenAI and Anthropic. It follows Google's confirmation that its Gemini model breached three companies during a security test, reported yesterday. OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman says the world is right to be afraid of artificial intelligence, but argues people should still trust the companies building it. Not everyone inside the industry agrees. The BBC reports multiple current and former workers at leading AI companies are sceptical that the technology could, in their words, kill everyone, a division within the sector itself.

Sport.

Rugby. All Blacks winger Mark Tele'a remains in contract limbo over a prospective move to England and has returned to play for Bay of Plenty in the NPC in the meantime, reported by Stuff. The Black Ferns face England at Twickenham this weekend in the WXV Global Series, coming off a sixty four to fourteen win over Scotland. Coach Allan Bunting says the side is ready to make a statement. England have brought in defence coach Shaun Edwards for the clash.

League. The Warriors' NRL finals exit continues to draw scrutiny. Former player Willie Poching says fatigue was behind the loss to the Knights in the semi-final. The NRL is reportedly set to drop referee Ashley Klein over a missed penalty call in the final minute.

Basketball. The Breakers lost eighty eight to eighty five to the Bullets in Brisbane, having led by double digits, a matter of record.

Netball. A Blues-linked bid has emerged for a new Auckland netball franchise, with talks underway for the Northern Mystics to join forces with the Blues Super Rugby side, reported by RNZ.

Rugby union. All Blacks captain Ardie Savea faces a lengthy spell on the sideline after shoulder surgery was confirmed following his dislocation against the Springboks.

Coming up. The Black Ferns play England at Twickenham this weekend. The Breakers continue their NBL campaign. The Constellation Cup between the Silver Ferns and Australia begins next month.

End of flash.
