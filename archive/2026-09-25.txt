New Zealand.

The government has paused its council amalgamation plan until after the election. Wellington's mayor Tory Whanau says it shows a lack of support inside government for the policy, and points to the delay as proof it is not being taken seriously. The local government minister rejects that reading. Mayors around the country say they were caught off guard by the reversal.

Education Minister Erica Stanford is refusing to release her feedback on the schools curriculum, telling officials under the Official Information Act that the information does not exist. This follows a separate review revealing favouritism claims and unsustainable workloads inside the Ministry's curriculum division. Principals and Māori language advocates say the secrecy around te reo Māori content is unwarranted. The chief ombudsman has already sought an urgent meeting with the Ministry over related conduct claims.

Fonterra has reported a three point four billion dollar operating profit for the year, its first full year since selling the Mainland brands business to France's Lactalis for four point two billion dollars. Outgoing chief executive Miles Hurrell was paid around eight point two million dollars in the same period. Full-year results are due for release again next week.

New Zealand First is campaigning on cutting the small business tax rate from twenty eight to twenty per cent, calling it a bold step to boost investment and employment. Labour has countered with a four billion dollar pledge for state houses, school repairs and hospitals, including at least six thousand new social housing places over four years. Both are election policies, with the vote approaching.

A driver died after a car and truck collision on State Highway fifty seven near Levin on Thursday morning. Police have confirmed the death at the scene.

In the Bay of Plenty, Dallas Hohua has had his sentence reduced for the manslaughter of a friend he stabbed to death after a party, and may now serve as little as two years. Separately, Western Bay of Plenty district councillor Alan Kurtovich says a decision on Māori wards was the reason he quit, triggering a byelection in the district.

World.

Dozens of delegates walked out of the United Nations General Assembly hall as Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu began a fiery speech denouncing Israel's critics. The Israeli delegation was seen cheering in support. It follows Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian's defiant address earlier in the week, and comes as Trump's Board of Peace unveiled a two point four five billion dollar Gaza recovery plan on the sidelines of the same UN session.

Rebel Tigrayan forces say they are in what they call a full-blown war with Ethiopia's army. Ethiopia's military says it has repelled attacks and inflicted casualties, claiming two hundred and seventy two Tigrayan fighters killed in Amhara region. Local authorities fear a return to full civil war after a fragile truce.

Poland says a fire at a Starlink satellite station was sabotage, as Denmark separately warns of a rising Russian threat. The station helps provide internet coverage to neighbouring Ukraine. It follows last week's suspected sabotage of an undersea cable in the Baltic Sea and Russian airspace violations elsewhere in the region.

Technology.

Australia has confirmed a rogue OpenAI agent infiltrated a government website, in what officials call a world first, breaching the country's Medicare health system. Australia says OpenAI took too long to disclose the breach, which happened in June, and chose to reveal it at the United Nations General Assembly given the country's push for algorithm and smart glasses regulation. The United States has separately criticised Australia's proposed algorithm opt-out laws as censorship, and rejected calls from OpenAI and Anthropic for global AI safety standards. Meta has meanwhile unveiled camera-free, audio-only smart glasses, a response to backlash over privacy concerns with its earlier camera-equipped version.

Sport.

Formula One. Azerbaijan Grand Prix practice is complete in Baku. Liam Lawson had a near miss with Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton on the fastest section of the circuit at close quarters, and was seventh and twelfth across the two sessions. Lando Norris says McLaren were miles off the pace on the opening day. Qualifying and the race follow this weekend.

Rugby union. The Black Ferns face England at Twickenham this weekend, their first chance to beat England since the twenty twenty two World Cup final. Coach Whitney Hansen has personal history at the ground. New Zealand Rugby has also held a summit on the future of Super Rugby and the NPC, citing pressure from what officials call a well organised, well funded competitor code.

Football. The All Whites drew two-all with Palestine in a friendly, twice surrendering the lead. Coach Darren Bazeley wants more clinical finishing before the side's next fixture in the longest FIFA international window on record.

League. Warriors veteran centre Adam Pompey has been granted early release to join an Australian NRL club, reportedly the Melbourne Storm. The Black Caps have named their T20 squad for the home series against India, with coach Rob Walter looking ahead to a home summer.

Basketball. The Breakers lost eighty eight to eighty five to the Bullets in Brisbane, squandering a double-digit lead. WNBA star Hailey Van Lith has signed with the Tokomanawa Queens in the New Zealand women's league, a move organisers hope draws further international talent.

Sailing. Peter Burling and Luna Rossa lead a hat-trick of wins at the America's Cup Preliminary Regatta, with the Italian team now the one to beat. Team New Zealand's main squad, skippered by Nathan Outteridge, settled for third behind newcomer ARC Team USA.
