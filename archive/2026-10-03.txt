New Zealand.

A magnitude five point three earthquake hit twenty five kilometres east of New Plymouth. More than seven thousand people reported feeling it, about three thousand describing moderate shaking. This is a matter of record from GeoNet.

National has unveiled a nine point energy plan called Electrify New Zealand two point zero, reviewing lines charges and adding a new affordability objective to tackle rising power bills. Labour has called it an absolute fizzer. The plan features in Saturday's finance debate between Nicola Willis and Barbara Edmonds, with the contest now shifting from fiscal restraint toward business needs.

Winston Peters dominated the first leaders' debate on airtime, speaking almost three times as much as newcomer Qiulae Wong, though commentators are split on who won the substance. Christopher Luxon walked back a claim about the Greens, then wrongly suggested the claim had come from the media. The Greens' tax pitch is drawing scrutiny as the party rides high in polling.

Liquidators have filed court proceedings against four former SolarZero directors, alleging reckless trading contributed to the solar firm's collapse. An estimated four hundred and seventy six million dollars is owed to unadjudicated creditors. Grant Thornton is pursuing the claims. A tradie owed money by the firm has welcomed the move.

In the Bay of Plenty, the Royal Australasian College of Surgeons says a chronic urologist shortage is affecting Tauranga and Nelson hospitals, which rely on regular locum staff. Health New Zealand is recruiting more nurses for Tauranga and Rotorua hospitals, following nurses there declaring a state of emergency earlier this year.

A large fire broke out at Farriers Bar and Eatery in Masterton, with seven crews sent to dampen flames and heavy smoke from the roof.

World.

France is facing growing disruption after riot police clashed with students in nationwide education protests. More than six hundred schools have been affected by demonstrations over overcrowded classrooms and teacher shortages, with the unrest now in its second week.

The G7 has agreed to release one hundred million barrels of oil and diesel reserves immediately, after President Trump threatened to ban United States diesel exports. United Kingdom diesel prices have already passed two pounds a litre for the first time, driven by the global price rises behind the threat. Europe has agreed to release what Trump called a massive amount from emergency stockpiles.

Rwanda backed rebels have pushed further into Goma in eastern Congo, with residents reporting continued gunfire and shelling after Congolese troops were overrun. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city, with no resolution in sight.

Russia's mayor of Kyiv, Vitaliy Klitschko, says intensified Russian strikes are tearing the capital apart, calling the situation very dramatic as Moscow continues hitting critical infrastructure.

Technology.

OpenAI has fired staff for what it called mishandling sensitive information, after the employees were investigated for sharing data with an outside AI evaluation group. The company separately unveiled a new assistant called Dots at its developer event, while delaying a planned new model over safety concerns.

The Bank of England's Andrew Bailey has warned that the scale of money flowing into artificial intelligence investment could trigger market shocks. He says the central bank is watching closely, but that regulating the technology itself is not the right place to start. Security firm Mindgard says Chinese models Kimi K2.6 and K3 Swarm were found in July able to bypass safety limits and give bioweapon related advice.
