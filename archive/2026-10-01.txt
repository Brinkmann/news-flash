New Zealand.

Adrian Rurawhe, former Speaker and the member for Te Tai Hauāuru for four terms, has died. Colleagues remember his connection to the Rātana movement and what they called a spine of steel. He served as Speaker in the last term of government.

Treasury's pre-election update forecasts a four billion dollar surplus for twenty twenty eight twenty nine, up from two point five billion in May. Finance Minister Nicola Willis says she is prioritising the return to surplus over tax cuts. National claims Labour's unfunded promises have reached eighteen point eight billion dollars, which Labour's finance spokesperson rejects.

Two bodies have been found at separate properties in Buller, with police treating both deaths as homicides. Postmortems will be carried out in Christchurch as police continue examining the properties.

A woman and her seven month old baby have been named as the two people killed in a crash in Peria, Northland, in August.

In the Bay of Plenty, Tauranga City Council's chief executive has resigned, with an employment lawyer telling RNZ the case highlights a weak safety culture across New Zealand workplaces. Western Bay of Plenty's Māori wards byelection, triggered by Alan Kurtovich's resignation over the council's wards decision, remains before voters.

New road rules take effect tomorrow, October the first, changing several driving requirements nationwide.

World.

A pilot has been arrested after allegedly stabbing another pilot and trying to crash a Flydubai flight bound for Israel. Passengers entered the cockpit and fought the attacker, with one telling Israel's prime minister he pulled the controls after the plane plummeted. The flight diverted to Saudi Arabia. Netanyahu praised the stabbed pilot, Captain Smit Machchhar, for saving lives.

Six people smugglers have been jailed for manslaughter over the deadliest Channel crossing disaster, in which thirty one migrants died in November twenty twenty one. A French court found the men were part of two smuggling networks.

Russia has launched its largest attack on Ukraine's energy infrastructure since spring, hitting eleven regions overnight. Ukraine's prime minister says residential buildings and railways were also targeted. Separately, Russia has warned NATO it would consider nuclear weapons if members tried to cut off its Kaliningrad exclave.

The last United States and United Kingdom troops have left Iraq, ending the coalition mission against Islamic State under a deal agreed two years ago, a key demand of the Iraqi government.
