New Zealand.

BNZ, ASB and Kiwibank have passed on Wednesday's quarter point official cash rate rise to two point seven five per cent, lifting floating mortgage rates. Finance Minister Nicola Willis says the Reserve Bank's assessment that recovery is broadening is welcome. About four thousand first home buyers now owe more than their homes are worth, according to figures reported today, with rising rates hitting recent buyers hardest.

Doctors at Middlemore Hospital say the emergency department is under severe pressure, with one hundred and three patients queuing at eleven fifteen on Wednesday night waiting on just seven doctors. A doctor compared the scene to overseas Covid footage. Under fives are bearing the brunt of this flu season, with Healthline fielding more than six hundred and eighty calls a day about flu like illness in that age group, and hospitalisations climbing sharply.

The Pacific Islands Forum has closed by condemning China's nuclear capable missile test, with every member except Nauru joining the rebuke and calling on all states testing intercontinental ballistic missiles to be transparent. Foreign Minister Winston Peters says Taiwan's observer status at next year's Forum, hosted by New Zealand, will follow a nineteen ninety two protocol, unchanged.

The Government's Gloriavale Christian School registration has been cancelled following a special Education Review Office review, though the school remains open pending a High Court challenge from its board. A barrister says the school should have been shut down decades ago. The Ministry of Education says closing a registered school is a significant statutory decision requiring due process.

Weather. A wind warning is in place for Wellington and gales up to one hundred and twenty kilometres an hour are forecast, with heavy rain and snow across the South Island through the weekend.

World.

The Reserve Bank of New Zealand aside, Nvidia has struck a twelve point nine billion dollar deal to buy the open source AI platform Hugging Face, giving it control of a major developer community. This follows Hugging Face's own systems being breached during an OpenAI test disclosed this week.

Rwanda backed rebels who entered Goma in the Democratic Republic of Congo have pushed further in, with the United Nations estimating more than one million displaced people are now trapped inside the city amid continuing gunfire and shelling.

The European Union and NATO are stepping up pressure on Russia after Germany blamed Moscow for a failed drone attack on Leipzig airport. NATO has also mounted its first coordinated response to suspected sabotage of an undersea cable in the Baltic Sea, after another cable was severed.

Feminist activist Gloria Steinem has died aged ninety two at her home in New York. Her foundation says she passed away peacefully. Steinem led the women's liberation movement in the United States and campaigned for abortion rights, same sex marriage and equal pay.

More than eighty migrants are feared dead after a boat was found off the Canary Islands with only forty four survivors on board. Rescuers say bad weather forced them to leave five bodies behind.

Technology.

Nvidia's acquisition of Hugging Face, detailed above, is the day's largest technology story and follows the AI safety incident disclosed this week involving OpenAI models and Hugging Face's systems.

Members of the House of Lords are calling for the United Kingdom government to have AI kill switch powers, saying they would provide a safety net against runaway systems such as those built by OpenAI and Anthropic. Separately, actors Matt Lucas and Hugh Bonneville are among performers calling for a law giving every person in the United Kingdom a legal right to own their voice, in response to AI voice cloning.

Motorsport. Kiwi Shane van Gisbergen says he will prioritise enjoying himself for the remainder of the NASCAR season, wanting to take more risks in his remaining races.
