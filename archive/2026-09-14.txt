New Zealand.

The All Blacks lost the final test of the series to South Africa forty three to twenty eight in Baltimore, handing the Springboks the Rugby's Greatest Rivalry trophy three one. Coach Dave Rennie said he was gutted, and had believed his side had a real chance. World media reaction has focused on South African dominance across the series. Rennie's contract position will be a talking point through the off season.

Auckland's City Rail Link had disruption on its first full weekday rush hour, with a fire caused by an electrical cable forcing delays and cancellations before services to Waitematā Station resumed. Prime Minister Christopher Luxon called the opening a major achievement. National has separately announced it would set up a dedicated Auckland Transport Police team of fifty new officers if re-elected, timed to the CRL's launch.

A survey of more than two hundred general practices found six in ten GPs remain worried about their financial viability, despite recent fee increases, and more than half now charge for services once free. A separate Stuff report says nearly half of practices are still short-staffed and looking for doctors. Health officials say the sector has begun to stabilise but call the recovery fragile.

The Ministry of Social Development faces continued criticism after a string of failures, including benefits wrongly suspended and thousands of families denied food grants. Newsroom's The Detail programme says apologies from the ministry have been thin despite the scale of the errors. Minister Louise Upston has previously accepted responsibility, and further review findings are expected.

A twenty-year-old University of Otago student died in 2023 after binge drinking and choking on vomit at his birthday party, a coroner has confirmed, with a post-mortem blood alcohol reading six times the legal driving limit. The coroner has issued a warning about prevalent youth drinking culture. Otago University says it continues reviewing student welfare policies.

A Tauranga man has been charged after political election hoardings were damaged in a vandalism spree in Papamoa. The case is before the courts. Separately, a homicide investigation has been launched in Rotorua after three people died at a house in Glenholme overnight, police confirm.

World.

Yemen's Houthi forces say they have seized control of the Bab al-Mandeb strait, a chokepoint for Red Sea shipping and Suez Canal traffic. More than two thousand Yemenis have fled fighting on the western coast into Djibouti. The world's top three container shipping operators say they remain wary of Red Sea routes despite Houthi pledges to end targeting of vessels.

A ferry capsized in the Java Sea off Indonesia, killing six people with one hundred and thirty still missing. Ships and helicopters continue the search. It follows scrutiny of a separate ferry sinking near Vanuatu, where a local MP has criticised authorities for a thirty-hour delay before search boats were deployed, saying more lives could have been saved.

BRICS leaders met in New Delhi for the final day of their summit, exposing divisions over the wars in Iran and Ukraine even as members agreed on the need for a new global order. New Zealand's Ministry of Foreign Affairs says a full-scale return to Iran-United States hostilities remains probably unlikely, though officials are watching fuel supply risks closely given New Zealand's exposure to any closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

Technology.

Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei has renewed his call for artificial intelligence development to slow down, after the company's threat intelligence report detailed a blocked attempt to use its models in biological weapons research. A former senior Anthropic researcher told the BBC AI staff are genuinely frightened for humanity's future. United States President Donald Trump downplayed the warnings, saying concerns about AI risk are overstated given competition with China. Some Silicon Valley executives and investors remain sceptical of the warnings.

Google will build a thirteen billion euro data centre in Finland, its largest single investment in Europe, expected to create tens of thousands of jobs. It comes as New Zealand political parties debate rules for new data centres here, with electricity demand from the sector forecast to triple by 2030 and parties still deciding how much new generation supply companies should be required to bring.

Sport.

League. The Warriors host the Newcastle Knights at Eden Park next Sunday in an NRL elimination semifinal, a sudden-death clash after their loss to the Dolphins. Fullback Taine Tuaupiki remains a doubt. Separately, the Warriors' NRLW season ended with a record defeat to the Broncos, who retained their premiership.

Formula One. Kimi Antonelli won the Spanish Grand Prix in Madrid after Lando Norris lost out to a badly timed virtual safety car, extending Antonelli's championship lead to eighty one points. Liam Lawson finished sixth, his best result of the season and the most points scored by a New Zealand driver in a single Formula One season.

Golf. Charley Hull and Lottie Woad led Europe to a fifteen and a half to twelve and a half Solheim Cup win over the United States at Bernardus Golf Club in the Netherlands. Shane Lowry won the Irish Open by eleven shots at Doonbeg, his first individual title since twenty twenty two.

Football. Manchester City beat Manchester United one nil in the derby at Old Trafford after Phil Foden was sent off and a controversial video review allowed Erling Haaland's winner to stand. Manchester United manager Michael Carrick said he was baffled by the decision.

Rugby. The Black Ferns beat France twenty eight to fourteen in their opening match of the WXV Global Series in Lyon, built on a strong defensive performance.

Athletics. New Zealand's pole vaulters narrowly missed the podium at the World Athletics Ultimate Championships in Budapest, where Britain's Josh Kerr won the men's fifteen hundred metres.

Coming up. The Warriors play Newcastle at Eden Park next Sunday in an NRL elimination semifinal. The Women's Softball World Cup qualifier continues in Oklahoma City through the week.
