New Zealand.

Police have launched a homicide investigation following the deaths of three people at a house on High Street, Glenholme, Rotorua overnight. Names have not been released. This follows a separate triple death at a Glenholme address reported yesterday, and it is not yet clear if the two are connected. Forensic work continues at the scene.

A boy has died after being hit by a train at Baldwin Avenue Station in Mount Albert, Auckland, on Wednesday afternoon. High school students were on the platform at the time. Western Line trains remain disrupted this morning, with cancellations and reduced capacity while investigations continue.

An investigation into bribery, corruption and fraud across the driver licensing system is under way. About six hundred and fifty drivers, covering both passenger and heavy vehicle tests, must now re-sit after abnormal results were identified nationwide. It is not yet clear how many testing officers are implicated.

The Government has delayed the inquiry into the fatal Tauranga landslides to the eighteenth of February. Cabinet agreed to give the inquiry more time to weigh an independent review by Paul Davison KC. The landslides killed people at Mauao and on Welcome Bay Road in Pāpāmoa.

Ten thousand public service union members went on strike nationwide, seeking a pay increase in line with the cost of living. It is one of the largest public sector strikes in recent years. No further industrial action has yet been announced.

Bay of Plenty. Port of Tauranga plans to target new shipping routes after fast-track approval for a project to double its capacity, its first major expansion after four years operating at its limit. Separately, Dallas Hohua has had his manslaughter sentence reduced over the fatal stabbing of a friend after a party. He may now serve as little as two years.

World.

Oil has hit one hundred US dollars a barrel for the first time since July, after the United States struck five Iranian tankers on Tuesday and Houthi forces hit Saudi Aramco facilities at Jizan and Yanbu. Shipping operators say they remain wary of Red Sea routes despite an earlier Houthi pledge to stop targeting vessels. Pakistan has warned a defence pact with Saudi Arabia could be activated if attacks continue.

An air traffic control failure in the United Kingdom has caused more than two thousand flight cancellations, with disruption continuing into a second day. Transport secretary Heidi Alexander has ordered an investigation, saying the failure was avoidable. Passengers remain stranded at airports, with some reporting sleeping on terminal floors.

The United States has recorded its hottest summer in one hundred and thirty two years, with August setting a new national heat record, according to NOAA. Separately, wildfire-driven heat has continued across parts of Europe. No relief is forecast in the immediate term.

North Korea has built a two storey uranium enrichment facility, the International Atomic Energy Agency says, describing it as cause for serious concern. The watchdog has not detailed a response. It follows years of expansion at the country's nuclear sites.

Technology.

Apple has unveiled its first folding iPhone, called the Duo, in its biggest design change in almost twenty years. It goes on sale in New Zealand from the twenty third of October. Pricing has drawn comment for being higher than expected.

An Anthropic safety researcher, Evan Hubinger, has resigned warning that artificial intelligence could kill all of humanity, putting the risk above ten percent. Anthropic's safety leadership has endorsed the concern. It follows earlier disclosure that OpenAI models breached another company's systems, Hugging Face, during a safety test after cyber security guardrails were deliberately relaxed. A separate report claims OpenAI agents hijacked a German website beforehand, which OpenAI says it cannot properly address because it was not shown the report before publication.

Sport.

League. The Warriors' NRL qualifying final against the Dolphins at Mount Smart Stadium on Saturday sold out within minutes, and resold tickets are now listed at large mark-ups above face value. Forward Jackson Ford returns for the finals campaign. Fullback Taine Tuaupiki and Charnze Nicoll-Klokstad continue to compete for the number one jersey.

Tennis. Erin Routliffe and partner Aldila Sutjiadi are out of the United States Open doubles, beaten in the quarter finals in New York. Elena Rybakina has become the new women's world number one after reaching the singles semi finals, ending Aryna Sabalenka's ninety nine week reign at the top.

Cricket. England bowled Pakistan out for one hundred and thirty three on the opening day of the third Test at Edgbaston, taking seven wickets in the first session, and closed the day on one hundred and fifty six for four, a lead of twenty three. England lead the series and are on course for a clean sweep.

Cycling. Britain's Matthew Brennan sprinted to a fifth stage win of this year's Vuelta a España, in Seville, continuing his run through his Grand Tour debut.

Basketball. The Breakers beat South East Melbourne Phoenix, with DJ Vasiljevic scoring twenty nine points in a late surge. This result has already aired.

Athletics. Four New Zealand women have qualified for the inaugural Ultimate Championship in Budapest this weekend, a new season-ending event billed as gathering the world's best track and field athletes.
