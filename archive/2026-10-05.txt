A heavy day of election policy leads this morning, with Labour's fiscal plan under fire and National committing one point seven five billion dollars to Auckland's Mill Road.

New Zealand.

Labour released its fiscal plan at the weekend, promising a pay rise for care and support workers and saying every election promise is paid for. National's Nicola Willis called it the least credible fiscal plan from a major party in living memory, and says it leaves ten billion dollars in questions unanswered. Labour is defending the numbers. An error page briefly appeared on Labour's website as the party tried to clarify its fiscal objective.

National has promised one point seven five billion dollars to build Mill Road in Auckland, one of its roads of national significance, if it leads the next government. Local councillor Daniel Newman backs the plan, but Greater Auckland says it is a sledgehammer to crack a nut. Elsewhere in the campaign, ACT pledged to remove tax on KiwiSaver earnings and to ban compulsory karakia in resource consents, the Greens promised free public transport for eligible groups and a two dollar fare cap for everyone else, and Te Pāti Māori confirmed a one person party list strategy. National MP Carlos Cheung says election signage was deliberately set on fire in Mount Roskill, and police have been told.

Two people are critical and one is in moderate condition after an early morning incident in Auckland's Glendene suburb. St John was called to a private address on Kirby Street around two thirty a.m. Monday. The circumstances have not been detailed.

Loafers Lodge survivors have been denied funding to attend the manslaughter trial opening in Wellington this week. Four people responsible for the building's fire safety systems face the High Court.

Tip Top has recalled a batch of Jelly Tip ice creams over possible bacterial contamination that can cause severe gastrointestinal illness. Affected buyers should return the product for a refund.

In the Bay of Plenty, Western Bay of Plenty councillor Alan Kurtovich has confirmed he quit over the council's Māori wards decision, triggering a byelection. He says the decision was absolutely right despite the cost to his seat. A man who stabbed a friend to death after a party, Dallas Hohua, has had his manslaughter sentence reduced and may serve as little as two years.

World.

Russia struck Kyiv's Pivnichnyi Bridge for a second consecutive day with a drone attack, as German Chancellor Friedrich Merz made a surprise visit to the capital and announced one point five billion US dollars in military and reconstruction aid. Ukraine says it is ready for US-backed trilateral talks with Russia, possibly this month in the United Arab Emirates.

Rwanda-backed rebels have pushed into the Congolese city of Goma, with residents reporting gunfire and shelling after government troops were overrun. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city.

A US Marine has been arrested over the alleged murder of a woman on Okinawa. Japan's government has lodged a formal protest with Washington, and the ambassador has vowed cooperation. The case has reignited tension over the American troop presence on the island.

Tens of thousands protested across Spain on Saturday over the government's failure to pass emergency housing legislation, with more than fifty separate demonstrations. Further protests are expected while the legislation remains stalled.

Technology.

OpenAI has fired staff for mishandling sensitive information, after an investigation found they shared data with an outside AI evaluation group. The company has not detailed what information was shared or how many were dismissed.

A security firm, Mindgard, says it found Chinese AI models Kimi K2.6 and K3 Swarm could be manipulated into bypassing their safety limits and giving bioweapon-related advice. The flaw was first identified in July and continues to draw scrutiny from researchers and the model's developers.

Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey has warned that the scale of cash flowing into artificial intelligence investment could trigger market shocks, and says regulating AI directly is not the right place to start. He wants rigorous testing and safeguards instead.

Sport.

Formula One. Max Verstappen won a delayed and chaotic Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia, Red Bull's first win of the year, after a software glitch left cars unable to handle a wet track and drivers compared the restart to a Lego race. George Russell retired. Liam Lawson's weekend continues to draw scrutiny over his Red Bull seat.

Rugby. Counties Manukau retained the Ranfurly Shield against Hawke's Bay, holding the Log o'Wood through summer. Blues hooker Kurt Eklund has retired on medical advice after a neck injury, ending his career as a former Maori All Blacks captain.

League. Dane Gagai has been cleared on appeal to switch his rugby league eligibility from Australia to New Zealand, and is available for the Kiwis' test against Australia in two weeks. The Sydney Roosters have won the NRL grand final, a Daly Cherry Evans field goal the difference, after Newcastle's Kalyn Ponga escaped a sin bin for a high shot that left the Roosters furious. In England, Wakefield Trinity beat Warrington Wolves twenty three to ten to win the Super League Grand Final, their first title since 1968.

Basketball. The Breakers beat Adelaide eighty eight to seventy two on the road, snapping their Australian slump. Guard Parker Jackson-Cartwright finished just short of a triple double.

Rally. Wales' Elfyn Evans has won his first World Rally Championship title, ending a twenty five year wait for a British champion, after rival Oliver Solberg crashed late in Rally Italia Sardegna.

No All Blacks or Super Rugby fixtures.
