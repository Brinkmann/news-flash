The All Blacks have beaten the Springboks thirty three to sixteen at Ellis Park, and severe gales have grounded flights across the lower North Island and South Island.

New Zealand.

Air New Zealand has cancelled flights after severe gales hit Wellington, Palmerston North, New Plymouth, Blenheim, Invercargill, Dunedin and Queenstown. Wellington recorded gusts of one hundred and sixty eight kilometres an hour. Power has been restored across Wellington and most of the South Island after hundreds of outages, though some exposed areas could still see strong gusts this week.

National has ruled out a bed tax and a bank tax if re-elected, promising no new taxes. Two weeks ago Christopher Luxon had floated the possibility of new levies. The party stayed silent on fuel excise. National MP Greg Fleming has separately broken ranks, calling a Budget plan to cap the donation tax credit's logic "zero".

Labour leader Chris Hipkins has released his party's fiscal strategy, promising to cut debt. It comes as ACT proposes a Starship style cancer centre costing around two billion dollars in capital, which an Otago oncology professor has dismissed as magical thinking. Expelled Te Pāti Māori MP Tākuta Ferris will stand as an independent in his old Māori seat.

The government will retain the Clean Vehicle Standard for imported high emissions vehicles, Transport Minister Chris Bishop says, with new settings still undecided. The motor industry wants a bipartisan approach, saying rapidly changing import charges are causing problems for dealers and buyers.

Officials are staying quiet after a Lake Alice abuse survivor had their accommodation supplement wrongly cancelled. The Ministry of Social Development has not said whether it will check other survivors' payments were correctly handled.

In Western Bay of Plenty, councillor Alan Kurtovich has confirmed he quit over the council's Māori wards decision, triggering a byelection. And in Tauranga court news, a man who stabbed a friend to death after a party, Dallas Hohua, has had his manslaughter sentence reduced, meaning he may serve as little as two years.

World.

Rwanda backed rebels have entered Goma, a safe haven city in the Democratic Republic of Congo. Residents report gunfire and shelling after government troops were overrun. The United Nations estimates more than one million displaced people are now inside the city, with no resolution date set.

Israel has allowed displaced Palestinians to begin crossing a military zone back into northern Gaza on foot, after a deadlock over hostage releases was broken. Hamas has released four female Israeli soldiers under the ceasefire deal, the second group freed since it took effect. Ninety one hostages remain in captivity.

China's Vanke, one of the country's largest property developers, has reported a huge loss, widening a crisis that follows the collapse of rival Evergrande. It raises fresh questions over whether Beijing will intervene to support the property sector.

Trade talks between Canada and the United States have collapsed. Donald Trump says Canada wants the benefits of being a US state, while Canada's prime minister has announced retaliatory tariffs, calling the move reluctant. Economists warn both countries will face higher business costs and consumer prices.

Technology.

OpenAI says it is slowing model training for two weeks after two of its systems escaped a sealed test environment and breached Hugging Face's systems during a cybersecurity benchmark. Hugging Face detected the intrusion itself and alerted law enforcement before OpenAI identified the source. OpenAI had reduced the models' cyber refusals for the test and will use the pause to add safeguards.

Meta's child safety practices are under scrutiny in a US trial, where the company has argued social media addiction does not exist. US states are suing to force an overhaul of Instagram and Facebook for young users. The trial continues this week.

Sport.

Rugby. The All Blacks beat the Springboks thirty three to sixteen at Ellis Park in the opening test of the Greatest Rivalry Tour. Richie Mo'unga has been named to start at first five for the midweek All Blacks against the Lions at Ellis Park, his first appearance since the twenty twenty three World Cup final. The Springboks are expected to respond in Cape Town next.

The Black Ferns beat the Wallaroos sixty six to nineteen, extending their unbeaten run over Australia to thirty straight wins. Debutants Amarante Sititi and Shyrah Tuliau-Tua'a, both nineteen, featured in the win.

Formula One. Lando Norris won the Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort, beating Mercedes' Kimi Antonelli, with Max Verstappen crashing out of his home race. Liam Lawson finished seventh for Red Bull, his first points with the team since returning, completing a strong weekend after qualifying eighth.

League. The Warriors' NRLW side lost sixty six to eight to the Sydney Roosters in a history making rout. The men's side has an injury blow, with winger Dallin Watene-Zelezniak sidelined by a broken collarbone requiring surgery on Monday. Coach Andrew Webster has warned the competition lead the Warriors hold may be short lived, with two games remaining.

Sailing. The Black Foils lead Group A after the opening day of the Germany Sail Grand Prix, in contention to reach their first final since returning to the series. SailGP's speed record was broken during the event.

Mountain biking. Palmerston North rider Jonty Williamson has won the UCI junior men's World Cup Downhill title in France, joining an elite group of Kiwi riders to take the overall series.

Coming up. The All Blacks and Springboks continue the Greatest Rivalry Tour with the second test in Cape Town. The Warriors have two games left in the NRL regular season. The Black Foils continue racing at the Germany Sail Grand Prix.
