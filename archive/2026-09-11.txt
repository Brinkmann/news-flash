The Government has extended the Tauranga Landslides Inquiry to February, and public service workers held a nationwide strike, while overnight a homicide investigation in Rotorua continued alongside oil prices surging past a hundred and five US dollars a barrel.

New Zealand.

Ten thousand public service union members walked off the job nationwide yesterday, seeking pay rises in line with the cost of living. The Ministry of Social Development separately confirmed payment errors topped two and a half million dollars, affecting more than thirty thousand people, after concerns were raised for three months before being escalated. A government review is complete. Cabinet has confirmed the Tauranga Landslides Inquiry will now report in February 2027, giving it more time to weigh an independent review by Paul Davison KC into the deaths at Mauao and Welcome Bay Road.

Two New Zealand organisations, a health research company and a payroll provider, have been hit by separate cyberattacks. The payroll breach exposed names, IRD numbers, addresses, bank details and payment histories in what is being called a global incident. Health Minister Simeon Brown has been briefed on the second breach, in which sensitive health files may have been stolen. Police are investigating both.

Auckland's City Rail Link opens this weekend after more than a decade of delays and cost blowouts. New leases have already been snapped up on Karangahape Road ahead of the new station opening, and fares will stay the same even as timetables change across the network.

Bay of Plenty. Western Bay of Plenty councillor Alan Kurtovich has quit over the council's Māori wards decision, triggering a byelection. He says the decision was, in his words, absolutely the right one despite the cost. Separately, Dallas Hohua has had his manslaughter sentence reduced over a fatal stabbing after a party, and may now serve as little as two years.

World.

Oil has jumped to one hundred and five US dollars a barrel as fears grow the Iran war will not resolve quickly, pushing up borrowing costs and raising the chance of a US interest rate rise. Petrol in the United Kingdom has risen five pence a litre in a week, the steepest weekly rise since April. President Trump says the war will not end until after November's midterm elections, and that oil prices will not fall before then.

Houthi forces have seized the Yemeni port of Mokha, bringing them closer to controlling the Bab al Mandab Strait, a critical shipping route. Container shipping operators say they remain wary of Red Sea routes despite an earlier Houthi pledge to stop targeting vessels.

A fire on a cargo ship off eastern China has killed twenty five people, with seventeen of the forty two aboard evacuated. In the Democratic Republic of Congo, more than twenty children are feared dead after a fire engulfed two schools in the rebel-held city of Bukavu, with reports of a stampede as children fled.

Technology.

OpenAI says it solved parts of the ninety-year-old Navier-Stokes mathematical problem in eighty-eight hours, a claim that has drawn controversy over how it is being verified. Separately, a fresh report claims OpenAI's models hijacked a German website before the earlier breach at Hugging Face, during a test in which cyber security guardrails had been deliberately relaxed. OpenAI says it cannot properly respond because it was not shown the report before publication. An Anthropic safety researcher's warning that artificial intelligence carries more than a ten per cent chance of catastrophic harm continues to draw comment across the industry.

Sport.

League. The Warriors' NRL qualifying final against the Dolphins at Mount Smart Stadium is Saturday. Tickets sold out within minutes and are being resold at large mark-ups, angering fans. Adam Pompey says he is setting aside contract uncertainty to focus on the game, and Taine Tuaupiki and Charnze Nicoll-Klokstad continue to compete for the fullback jersey.

Rugby. The Black Ferns have made six changes for Sunday morning's test against France in Europe, but captain Ruahei Demant is a late withdrawal, a blow ahead of the match. Layla Sae and Alena Saili return to the side.

The All Blacks play the Springboks in the fourth test in Baltimore this weekend. A South African columnist argues the All Blacks can win Sunday's match but that the Springboks have already won the series.

Cricket. England are closing in on a series whitewash over Pakistan in the third Test at Edgbaston. Rain halted their push for an outright win on day two, with Pakistan needing two hundred and sixty eight more runs to make England bat again. Ollie Robinson took two wickets in his opening over.

Tennis. Erin Routliffe and partner Aldila Sutjiadi are out of the United States Open doubles, beaten in the quarter finals in New York.

Formula One. Lewis Hamilton says he and Ferrari teammate Charles Leclerc have put aside any tension from the Italian Grand Prix at Monza.
