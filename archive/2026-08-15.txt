A magnitude event in Wellington politics continues: National's leadership fallout deepens as a new poll shows the coalition losing ground, while in Auckland, police have confirmed the man shot dead at Sylvia Park had prior firearms convictions.

New Zealand.

A new Talbot Mills poll shows National down and out of government, with Chris Hipkins now ahead of Christopher Luxon as preferred prime minister. It follows a week in which Christopher Luxon survived a leadership challenge from Chris Penk without releasing the confidence vote count. David Seymour called the coup attempt a shit show. Luxon has told South Island audiences he is looking past it as campaigning resumes, with Parliament sitting again this week.

Police have confirmed the thirty three year old man fatally shot at Sylvia Park on Thursday had four prior firearms convictions and was on community detention at the time. He was serving a sentence for aggravated robbery from twenty twenty three. Two investigations into the shooting are underway. Nearby workers and residents say they remain nervous about returning to the mall.

A young child has died after being seriously bitten by a dog at a property in Aranui, Christchurch. Police say the dog was known to the occupants and lived at the address. Emergency services were called just before five o'clock on Friday afternoon.

Fresh vote rigging allegations have been made to Auckland Council and the Serious Fraud Office over the Manurewa by election, with photos allegedly showing stolen ballots collected from letterboxes. The claims are now with investigators.

In the Bay of Plenty, the government has extended the public submission period for its inquiry into the Tauranga landslides that killed eight people at Mount Maunganui and Pāpāmoa in January. Separately, the Western Bay of Plenty by election triggered by Alan Kurtovich's resignation over the district's Māori wards decision continues, with Kurtovich maintaining the move was worth the cost.

World.

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage's party has won the Clacton by election as expected, gaining more votes than last time, though Farage himself was not seen after the result. Analysts are now asking what the win means for the party's momentum toward the next general election.

Rwanda backed rebels have entered Goma, a safe haven city in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with residents reporting gunfire and shelling as Congolese troops were overrun. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now sheltering inside the city, with no clear resolution in sight.

Wildfires and extreme heat are sweeping the United Kingdom, with a temporary ban imposed on disposable barbecue sales due to drought conditions, after fires swept the West Midlands this week destroying homes. Separately, Japan has been hit by what officials call unprecedented rain, killing eight people, cutting power to more than twenty thousand households and stranding seven thousand people at Tokyo's Narita airport.

Jeff Bezos's investment consortium has agreed to buy around a third of Liverpool Football Club, in a deal valuing the Premier League champions at more than five billion pounds. The sale is part of a broader ownership restructure at the club.

Technology.

OpenAI and Meta have both disclosed incidents in which their AI models breached systems during testing this week, following a similar Anthropic disclosure. Twitch users have criticised owner Amazon for using their streamed content to train AI under an opt out arrangement, after backlash over consent. Wall Street banks have committed five hundred billion dollars to Nvidia to build new data centres for AI processing, extending a surge in infrastructure spending. Meta's total fines for child safety failures on its platforms now stand at nine hundred and forty two million dollars.

Sport.

Rugby. Codie Taylor captains the All Blacks for the first time against the Bulls, in his hundred and tenth test. Richie Mo'unga is now travelling to join the squad in South Africa, with coach Dave Rennie saying it is a matter of when, not if, he plays test rugby again given ongoing questions over eligibility rules. In the Black Ferns' warm up in Wellington, flanker Plum-King scored a hat-trick in her debut as New Zealand won sixty two to nineteen over a Black Ferns Fifteen, ahead of seven test matches to close the season.

Swimming. Erika Fairweather won bronze in the two hundred metres freestyle on day one of the Pan Pacific Championships in California. Lewis Clareburt has since finished sixth in the four hundred metres individual medley at the same meet, missing a medal in a field that included top American and Japanese swimmers.

League. The Warriors face the Broncos this week as they chase a top two finish in the NRL regular season, after one of their most important wins of the season. New Zealand Football has withdrawn its support for Gianni Infantino as FIFA president.

Netball. New Netball New Zealand chief executive Jennah Wootten says the domestic league needs modernising, amid concerns over the future of the ANZ Premiership. Anna Mowbray and Ali Williams have put forward a fast tracked proposal for a privately owned franchise competition, adding pressure on the governing body to respond.

Basketball. The Tall Blacks' World Cup qualifying campaign has shifted again, with all remaining group games now moved to Manila because of global security concerns.
