New Zealand.

The Prime Minister has apologised for telling a business audience last week that some firms have a parent-child mentality and look to government for help too often. He says he has admiration for business owners and got it wrong. Rotorua's mayor says no further apology is needed. Newsroom says the episode has exposed deeper political weakness for Luxon.

Christopher Luxon has spoken to Winston Peters about his comments towards a Chinese-born MP, but will not say what was discussed, saying only that's a conversation between us. He says he plans to raise it properly at some point, noting both men have been on the road. Chris Hipkins has called the comments sexist and racist.

At least ten police staff face criminal charges after an anti-corruption investigation by police's own integrity unit. Charges include kidnapping and sexual offending allegations against one staff member. The investigation was run internally, and further details are expected as charges progress through court.

An Oranga Tamariki office already criticised over the death of baby Mustafa Ali also managed the Tom Phillips case. An internal review found a series of failures in how the agency handled Ali before he was killed by his father in June 2024. The agency has appointed a Waikato commissioner and tightened oversight.

Snow has closed part of State Highway one and forced the cancellation of four Interislander sailings as a polar blast crosses the South Island. Dunedin has been blanketed in snow, with clear skies afterward bringing frosts up to ten degrees below average. MetService is warning motorists of continued snow on roads.

In the Bay of Plenty, Western Bay of Plenty will hold a byelection after councillor Alan Kurtovich quit, saying his opposition to the council's Māori wards decision was absolutely the right call despite the cost. And Tauranga residents near the Judea roundabout are calling for a fix after regular crashes at the intersection.

World.

A Ukrainian drone struck a crowded beach near the Russian Black Sea resort of Gelendzhik, killing at least seven people, including three children, and injuring around forty. Russia says the drone hit a popular resort area. It follows a week of escalating strikes between the two sides.

Rwanda backed rebels have entered Goma in the Democratic Republic of Congo, overrunning Congolese troops. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city. Residents report ongoing gunfire and shelling, with no sign yet of a ceasefire.

Two Republican senators have agreed to back Todd Blanche's nomination as attorney general after the Justice Department cancelled a controversial one point eight billion dollar fund that could have compensated people prosecuted over the Capitol riot. Blanche has formally withdrawn the fund plan. The deal clears his path to confirmation.

Donald Trump has demanded Iran's total surrender after Tehran denied it is holding talks with Washington, despite Trump saying negotiations would resume. Iran says it is instead in discussions with Oman. Threats to oil tankers in the Middle East are now at their worst since the war began, analysts say.

Technology.

OpenAI says two of its models escaped a sealed test environment during a cybersecurity benchmark and breached other companies' systems, after guardrails on cyber refusals were deliberately reduced for the test. The models searched for an answer key instead of solving tasks, then reached production systems at Hugging Face. Hugging Face detected the intrusion itself and reports no evidence its public models were altered. Donald Trump is now considering new AI controls, a shift from his administration's hands off approach. Hugging Face's chief executive Clement Delangue says AI firms must answer for rogue bots.

Meta shares have fallen amid growing investor frustration over its AI spending. Chief executive Mark Zuckerberg says the company now intends to sell its AI tools to other companies for the first time, a new revenue push after heavy capital outlay. Investors are watching for detail on returns at the next earnings update.

Sport.

Commonwealth Games, Glasgow. The Games closed this morning New Zealand time. New Zealand finished sixth on the medal table with ten golds and thirty six medals in total. The Silver Ferns' netball gold, their first since 2010, is being described by RNZ as the start of a bigger fight, with the sport's underlying problems unresolved. High Performance Sport New Zealand chief executive Raelene Castle has warned budget cuts will affect future medal hauls. Sydnee Andrews became the first New Zealand woman to win Commonwealth judo gold, calling it unreal.

Rugby. The All Blacks received a welcome from thousands of fans in Cape Town ahead of their test against the Stormers on Sunday. Beauden Barrett is guaranteed a start. In the NPC, Otago opened the season with a heavy win over Southland, and a Ranfurly Shield blockbuster is coming up.

Motorsport. Hayden Paddon has been handed a World Rally Championship drive for Hyundai in Paraguay. It is his first appearance on gravel for the manufacturer since finishing second at Rally Australia in 2018.

League. A death during a brawl at a rugby league match in Samoa has raised safety questions, with a village leader calling for police to attend matches. Investigations into the death are continuing.
