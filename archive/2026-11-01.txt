A magnitude five point nine... no, today's lead: the Kiwi Ferns have reached the Rugby League World Cup semi-finals, Porirua's deputy mayor Kylie Wihapi has died, and Fonterra's milk price lift adds to a week of economic news.

New Zealand.

The Kiwi Ferns beat Papua New Guinea to reach the Rugby League World Cup semi-final, trailing twelve to eight at halftime before scoring thirty two unanswered points. They next play Samoa. The Kiwis men's team also beat Fiji to reach the semi-finals, avenging a quarter-final loss at the twenty seventeen World Cup.

Porirua deputy mayor Kylie Wihapi has died after a fall on Saturday. She was the city's first elected Māori ward councillor. Mayor Anita Baker confirmed her death.

The Waitangi Tribunal says the Crown should apologise after blindsiding Māori over a decision to remove school boards' obligations to give effect to the Treaty. It found the process fell short of the standard of honour required, landing in the final week of the election campaign. Election day is Saturday the seventh of November.

The Electoral Commission is investigating a Split Your Vote website run by Harry Mowbray, who says he had no sign off from National, ACT or New Zealand First. National, ACT and New Zealand First are also at odds over a separate vote splitting campaign funded by Reality Check Radio and written by Don Brash. Separately, the Commission says enrolment changes may have wrongly affected up to six thousand eight hundred people with residence class visas.

Fonterra has lifted its farmgate milk price forecast midpoint by twenty five cents, a second rise in a week, on stronger global milk powder demand. It benefits dairying regions including Waikato and the Bay of Plenty. The Education Review Office says almost two hundred schools are rated of concern or worse, about eight percent of the country's schools, concentrated in the upper North Island.

In Western Bay of Plenty, Dallas Hohua, convicted of manslaughter for stabbing a friend to death after a party, has had his sentence reduced and may now serve as little as two years.

World.

Rwanda backed rebels have entered Goma, a safe haven city in the Democratic Republic of Congo. Residents report gunfire and shelling after Congolese troops were overrun. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city.

More than three hundred and twenty thousand people protested across France for a third consecutive weekend over the cost of living and the state of public services, with clashes reported between police and demonstrators in Paris.

Israel has allowed displaced Gazans to begin crossing a military zone that bisects the enclave on foot, after a deadlock over hostage releases was broken. It follows the release of four more female Israeli hostages under the ceasefire deal, with ninety one hostages still held by Hamas, including at least thirty four bodies.

A suspected sabotage of a deep sea cable in the Baltic Sea has triggered NATO's first coordinated response to attacks on undersea infrastructure, after another cable was severed.

Technology.

Binance is trying to recover six point three billion US dollars stolen in a hack it calls the largest of its kind on record. Two analytics firms have attributed the attack to a North Korean hacking unit known as TraderTraitor, though there is no official government confirmation. Binance says it has covered ninety percent of customer losses from its own funds.

Alphabet, Google's parent company, has lost about five hundred billion US dollars in market value after revealing its own artificial intelligence search summaries are cutting into advertising revenue. The stock hit a six month low, part of a broader technology sell off with the Nasdaq at its lowest level in more than two months amid concern spending on artificial intelligence, nearing one trillion US dollars, has outrun returns.

Sport.

Formula One. Kiwi driver Liam Lawson and Max Verstappen both received grid penalties for the Mexican Grand Prix after colliding in practice, leaving debris on track, with each blaming the other for the incident.

Golf. New Zealand amateur Zack Swanwick is on the verge of a historic win, leading into the final round of the Asia Pacific amateur championship at Te Arai Links.

Rugby. The Black Ferns completed a series clean sweep of France, winning ten of their eleven tests this year, including all four against the French. Kaipo Olsen Baker has been named the world's best women's rugby player, the first Black Fern to win the award since Ruahei Demant in twenty twenty two.

Cricket. The Black Caps posted a record Twenty20 total against India at Eden Park, having trailed three nil in the series. In South Africa, the Proteas bowled Australia out for one hundred and eleven to win the test series, their first since the twenty eighteen ball tampering scandal.

Football. Auckland FC conceded a stoppage time goal to draw one all with Brisbane Roar. The Wellington Phoenix beat Newcastle Jets to move to three wins from three, guaranteeing them top of the table after round three.

Basketball. The Breakers lost one hundred and eight to one hundred and one to the Adelaide Thirty Sixers at home.

Boxing. Kiwi cruiserweight David Nyika's world title fight has been put on hold after a legal challenge from his rival forced a change to his December plans.

Cycling. New Zealand paracyclists won three more gold medals at the UCI Para Cycling Track World Championships in the Netherlands.

Coming up. Rugby League World Cup semi-finals continue this week, the Kiwi Ferns play Samoa, and the Kiwis play Fiji. The Mexican Grand Prix grid carries penalties for Lawson and Verstappen. Election day is Saturday the seventh of November.
