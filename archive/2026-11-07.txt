New Zealand.

It is election day. Polls close at seven o'clock tonight. Under election day rules, campaigning and new advertising must stop, and results begin coming in once voting ends. Special votes, which include anyone voting outside their electorate, will not be fully counted for about two weeks, so final seat numbers may shift after tonight. Winston Peters put his own ballot in the wrong box during advance voting, and a last minute poll has Labour climbing out of the thirties, leaving the race on a knife edge.

Severe thunderstorms are possible across the North Island today, with rain up to forty millimetres an hour raising flash flooding risk. Voters are being told to check radar before heading to polling places.

Porirua has farewelled Deputy Mayor Kylie Wihapi, who died after a fall at her home last weekend, aged forty five. Her funeral was held this morning at Te Rauparaha Arena.

One person has died and four were taken to hospital after a single vehicle crash on Brandon Street in central Wellington in the early hours of Saturday. Police are asking motorists to avoid the area.

In Western Bay of Plenty, councillor Alan Kurtovich has confirmed his resignation was triggered by the council's Māori wards decision, forcing a byelection. He says the decision was, in his words, absolutely the right one despite the cost.

Dallas Hohua, who stabbed a friend to death after a party, has had his manslaughter sentence reduced and may serve as little as two years.

Wall Street's sharp sell off has so far left New Zealand shares largely unaffected, with the NZX holding up even as Japanese and South Korean markets fell further. Separately, property listings hit their highest October level in twelve years, with national asking prices down two point seven percent.

World.

Donald Trump has named Stephen Miller as his new White House chief of staff, replacing Susie Wiles, days after Republicans suffered defeats in the midterms. Miller is considered the architect of Trump's hardline immigration policy and has been a close Trump adviser since twenty sixteen. Wiles called serving in the role the honour of her lifetime. Miller starts Monday.

Meghan Markle's estranged father, Thomas Markle, has been arrested in the United States over alleged child sexual abuse images, according to Associated Press sources. Authorities say sexually explicit photos appearing to show him with prepubescent girls were found. Markle previously said she was deeply distressed by the allegations.

Global stocks fell further overnight as fears about an artificial intelligence spending slowdown spread from the United States to Asia and Europe, following last week's heavy falls in tech shares. Investors are reacting to expectations of tougher scrutiny of data centre construction.

A new wave of hoax bomb threats has hit European airlines this week. No link has been established to the earlier threats against media outlets, and no explosives have been found.

Technology.

Elon Musk has signalled the end of Tesla's Cybertruck, saying production in Texas will stop to make room for the company's driverless Cybercab taxis. Musk spent close to five hundred million US dollars on the twenty twenty six midterms, an individual spending record, without delivering the Republican wins he backed.

Binance says the record six point three billion US dollar hack of its exchange used fake job interviews, with an engineer contacted through a legitimate job hunting platform and invited to bogus interviews with fake company staff. North Korea has denied involvement, despite US agencies and cyber analysts blaming Pyongyang.

A cyber attack has hit supermarket freezers across Europe, with fridges in Ireland, Sweden and Belgium reportedly having their temperatures altered. France's blood service also says hackers affected cold storage rooms holding blood supplies, with the culprits still unclear.

Sport.

Rugby. The All Blacks face Scotland at Murrayfield in Edinburgh on Saturday, kicking off early Sunday morning New Zealand time. Jordie Barrett returns at centre, with Fabian Holland and Scott Barrett continuing as the first choice locks. Coach Dave Rennie says no one wants to be part of the first All Blacks side to lose to Scotland, calling it legacy rather than fear.

Rugby league. The Kiwis and Kiwi Ferns play World Cup semifinals in Newcastle on Saturday. The Kiwis face Tonga in a win or go home match, with Kalyn Ponga in line to play a key role.

Tonga have beaten Romania forty three to thirty eight in a World Rugby Nations Cup test in Bucharest.

Formula One. Liam Lawson heads into the Sao Paulo Grand Prix weekend still without clarity on his twenty twenty seven seat. Racing Bulls has signed a contract extension for a team staff member while Lawson's own future remains unresolved. The constructors' title race is settled, but the Sao Paulo weekend opens the contest for next season's grid.

Football. The Wellington Phoenix face Auckland FC this weekend, their eighth derby of the season, nine months after a five nil loss prompted their former coach to quit.

Basketball. The New Zealand Breakers have lost a fifth straight National Basketball League match, leaving them bottom of the standings.
