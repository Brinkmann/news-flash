RNZ faces sweeping job cuts after revealing a near twelve million dollar budget blowout, as the government rules out financial help.

New Zealand.

RNZ chief executive Paul Thompson is under pressure to go early after the broadcaster revealed an eleven point eight million dollar deficit, caused by what the board calls almost incomprehensible financial errors. Fifty eight roles face cuts. Media Minister Paul Goldsmith has ruled out a bailout, calling the blowout pretty shocking. Chris Hipkins says he is concerned for RNZ's future. An all staff meeting has been called as the delayed annual report is finalised.

Parliament is in transition after last week's election. Christopher Luxon held an hour long meeting with Opportunity leader Qiulae Wong, despite ruling out working with her party during the campaign. Outgoing National MP Chris Bishop criticised vote splitting campaigns as a distraction, and fellow departing MP Vanessa Weenink said the party had gone too far to the right. Thirty five new MPs are headed to Wellington. Coalition talks continue.

Severe thunderstorm warnings remain in force for the North Island, with up to one hundred and thirty millimetres of rain forecast. Auckland is under a twenty six hour heavy rain watch, and Bay of Plenty is under an orange warning, with flash flooding possible.

In Western Bay of Plenty, the byelection triggered by councillor Alan Kurtovich's resignation over the Māori wards decision is proceeding. Ruapehu's mayor has dismissed fears over power demand from a proposed data centre and pellet factory at the old Winstone Pulp site as scaremongering, as the plan is unveiled.

Maketū Pies has closed after its owner failed to find a buyer, with twenty one full time equivalent jobs lost.

World.

Nvidia shares tumbled after Donald Trump said the FBI is investigating an affair between chief executive Jensen Huang and a woman alleged to be a Chinese spy. Hundreds of billions of dollars were wiped from Nvidia's value before AI stocks partly recovered. Trump says he does not believe Huang knew of any security risk.

Ukraine peace talks resume in the United Arab Emirates, with Kyiv, Washington and Moscow sending delegations for the first trilateral talks since February. US officials say there is some flexibility on both sides.

A steel plant explosion in Aliaga, Turkey, has killed at least seventeen people after a crane collapsed into a furnace. President Erdogan has ordered an investigation.

Russell Brand has been sentenced to ten years and six months in prison after being convicted of rape and sexual assault following a four week trial. His former partner Jemima Goldsmith has separately accused him of violence and coercive control.

Technology.

Global share markets fell sharply then partly recovered last week on fears of an artificial intelligence spending slowdown, with United States midterm results fuelling expectations of tougher scrutiny of data centre construction. Analysts say concerns about the industry's spending had been building for some time before the selloff.

France has accused Iran of hacking supermarket fridges, spoiling tonnes of chilled and frozen food last month after cold storage units heated up. The French Blood Establishment says hackers also affected cold rooms storing blood supplies.

Rocket Lab's third quarter revenue rose to just under four hundred and seventy six million dollars, up about sixty million on the previous quarter, and ahead of its own guidance. The company also reported its first ever positive cash flow.

Sport.

Formula One. Liam Lawson is reportedly closing in on a new deal for next season, with a decision expected before the Las Vegas Grand Prix. He has again picked up points without yet taking his first podium.

Rugby League World Cup. The Jillaroos beat Fiji thirty to fourteen in Sydney to reach the women's final. Toa Samoa's Spencer Leniu faces a ban after a money gesture saw him sent off in the men's semifinal, reigniting debate over league pay. The 2030 World Cup is headed to the United States, with games spread across twelve thousand kilometres and up to seventeen time zones.

Rugby. All Blacks fullback Beauden Barrett brought up two milestones in the win over Scotland. Jordie Barrett and Will Jordan remain doubtful for the test against Wales in Cardiff this weekend after injuries picked up at Murrayfield.

Squash. Kiwi Paul Coll beat Peruvian Diego Elias to win his second United States Open title in Philadelphia, rallying from a set down.

Basketball. The Breakers have lost star point guard Parker Jackson-Cartwright for the season with a lower leg injury, a blow club management calls devastating.

Supercars. Kiwi Matt Payne takes a win into this week's championship decider, his first win since August after finishing second at Sandown on Saturday.

Coming up. Coalition talks continue in Wellington. The Rugby League World Cup finals are Sunday night in Brisbane. The All Blacks play Wales in Cardiff this weekend. Severe weather warnings remain current for the North Island today.
