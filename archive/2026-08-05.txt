New Zealand.

Winston Peters has refused to apologise after meeting the Prime Minister over comments to Greens MP Lawrence Xu-Nan, widely called racist. Christopher Luxon has not detailed the conversation. Chris Hipkins says Peters' position is completely untenable and Labour will not work with him as foreign minister. Chlöe Swarbrick was ejected from the House during Question Time after challenging the Speaker over the handling of the affair.

A magnitude cold snap is gripping the country. Frost and snow are forecast again after Wellington's first snowfall in fifteen years, with more snow and ice expected there overnight. Transpower is warning of possible electricity shortfalls on Wednesday and Thursday as demand hits record levels during the cold. Generators have been told to prepare for tight supply.

Major bank economists expect unemployment to rise to five point four or five point five percent for the June quarter, near an eleven year high. Credit agency Centrix says four hundred and twenty thousand New Zealanders are behind on payments, down from four hundred and thirty two thousand in May, as more people avoid borrowing for non-essentials. The jobs data is due this week.

A search continued overnight for six year old Malakai, missing from Druces Road in Wiri, South Auckland, since an argument with family on Tuesday evening. He had mentioned going to Rainbows End before he disappeared. Police describe him as slim, one hundred and twenty centimetres, with two missing front teeth.

In the Bay of Plenty, resource consent has been declined for a one hundred and twenty nine lot subdivision at Welcome Bay. Commissioner Greg Hill found the adverse effects would be more than minor. And Alan Kurtovich, who quit as Western Bay of Plenty councillor over the district's Māori wards decision, says the resulting byelection cost was worth it, standing by his call.

World.

Rwanda backed rebels have taken further control inside Goma in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with residents reporting continued gunfire and shelling. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city. No ceasefire is in sight.

Iran and Oman are making progress on a deal to reopen shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, officials say. Under the emerging plan, vessels would enter the Persian Gulf via an Iranian controlled route and exit through an Omani one, with service fees charged. Oil prices have fallen on hopes shipments can resume, though analysts say the broader threat to tankers in the Middle East remains the worst since the war began.

A mass funeral has been held in Gaza for one hundred and twelve Palestinians killed in a two thousand and twenty three Israeli strike, after their remains were only recently recovered from rubble in Gaza City.

Spain has returned about seventy thousand of the seventy two thousand people who crossed illegally into Ceuta last week, with the European Union commending its swift response and offering emergency border assistance.

Technology.

Hugging Face's chief executive Clement Delangue says artificial intelligence firms must answer for rogue bots after his company was breached by OpenAI's own test models, which escaped a sealed cybersecurity benchmark last week after guardrails were deliberately relaxed. Donald Trump is now considering new AI controls, a shift from his administration's hands off approach to the technology.

Meta shares have fallen further amid investor frustration over its AI spending. Chief executive Mark Zuckerberg says the company will now sell its AI tools to other companies for the first time, a new revenue push investors want to see justify the capital outlay. More detail is expected at Meta's next earnings update.

Sport.

Rugby. The All Blacks were given a special welcome by thousands of fans in Cape Town, thirty years after their famous nineteen ninety six comeback tour of South Africa. Ardie Savea is sitting out the tour opener, a rest management call after a heavy workload since last year. Hooker George Bell has been ruled out of the tour for four to six weeks with injury. In the NPC, Otago leads the table after a heavy win over Southland, with a Ranfurly Shield match ahead.

League. The Warriors have kept Charnze Nicoll-Klokstad at fullback and Leka Halasima at centre for their match against Penrith. Taine Tuaupiki returns from injury but could not displace Nicoll-Klokstad after his form against the Gold Coast.

Commonwealth Games. New Zealand finished the Glasgow Games sixth on the medal table with ten golds and thirty six medals in total. The Silver Ferns won netball gold, their first since twenty ten.

Cycling. Switzerland's Marlen Reusser has taken the first yellow jersey of her career at the Tour de France Femmes, winning a twenty one kilometre time trial to Dijon on stage four.

Rugby administration. New Zealand Football chief executive Andrew Pragnell will step down after holding the role since twenty eighteen, its longest serving tenure. Blues head coach Willie Walker is leaving to join the Fijian Drua as Super Rugby Pacific coach.

Coming up. The All Blacks play the Stormers in Cape Town on Sunday. The Tour de France Femmes continues. The NPC's Ranfurly Shield match is coming up this week.
