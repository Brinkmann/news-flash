The Reserve Bank's surprise decision to hold the Official Cash Rate sends the New Zealand dollar sharply lower, and a new poll puts National below its 2020 result nine days before the election.

New Zealand.

The Reserve Bank held the Official Cash Rate at two point seven five percent on Wednesday, a split decision that surprised economists who had expected a rise. The New Zealand dollar dropped close to a cent against the United States dollar within minutes. Economists say home loan rates could now ease, a relief for borrowers but a blow for savers.

The RNZ to Reid Research poll has National at twenty four point eight percent, below its previous low from the 2020 election. Opportunity rose to seven point two percent, a new high in this poll series. Finance Minister Nicola Willis called the result "out of whack". Winston Peters described this as the worst campaign he has seen, and has publicly backed National's Chris Bishop over his own New Zealand First candidate in Hutt South, telling supporters there it was not an endorsement of National as a whole.

Rodney Fallowfield, already jailed in 2021 for manslaughter over his wife's death on honeymoon, has pleaded guilty at the High Court in Christchurch to murdering two men and attempting to murder a third.

Health New Zealand has issued free needle exchange kits after a surge in people injecting peptides, handing out twenty three hundred kits to one hundred and ninety pharmacies in six weeks. Officials say gym users and even parents seeking peptides for children are among those affected.

In Western Bay of Plenty, Alan Kurtovich has confirmed his council resignation was driven by the district's Māori wards decision, triggering the byelection now under way. In Kawerau, Dallas Hohua, convicted of manslaughter for stabbing a friend to death after a party, has had his sentence reduced and may serve as little as two years.

A five theatre, eighty million dollar private hospital, Tauranga Haumoana, is planned for 2029. Tauranga Hospital currently has eight operating theatres.

World.

A high speed train collision south of Madrid has killed at least sixty eight people and injured more than one hundred, Spain's worst rail disaster in over a decade. All Spanish Copa del Rey matches were suspended Wednesday as a mark of respect. The cause is still under investigation.

Israel's election has produced no clear winner. With eighty five percent of votes counted, Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud bloc leads with fifty three seats in the one hundred and twenty seat Knesset, short of a majority. Analysts say a new election may be the likeliest outcome.

Britain's Chancellor John Healey delivered his first Budget, including a one hundred pound payment toward energy bills from January and a bank tax, alongside a one percent spending cut ordered across departments to fund defence investment. Tax thresholds remain frozen for three more years.

Flavio Bolsonaro has landed in Miami after his father's ally lost Brazil's presidential election, ending speculation over his whereabouts after he failed to surrender his passport as ordered.

Technology.

Anthropic's initial public offering prospectus, running to three hundred and fifty five pages, has disclosed new detail on the company including a warning of what it calls catastrophic risk from artificial intelligence as it prepares to become the first major AI company to list publicly.

A global cyber security scramble over a critical flaw in the PHP programming language continues, now several days in. The flaw was uncovered with the help of artificial intelligence tools. Programmer Ondřej Surý, whose patches have been central to the response, says the pace of work has been relentless. Britain's government says it is investing one hundred million pounds in a new AI biosecurity lab.

Sport.

Cricket. The Black Caps face India in the third Twenty20 international at Eden Park on Friday night, a sellout. India's Abhishek Sharma scored a century off thirty five balls in the series, described as one of the innings of the tournament.

League. The Rugby League World Cup Kiwis remain without confirmation on Kalyn Ponga, who is still a chance to feature further in the tournament despite injury. Separately, Australian Rugby League has rejected New Zealand Rugby's legal challenge over the PNG Chiefs name, saying it will keep using it in the NRL.

Rugby. All Blacks coach Dave Rennie says his side is still chasing the consistency and ruthlessness that marked his teams previously, ahead of further matches on the northern tour.

Netball. The Silver Ferns' eleven match winning streak has ended, beaten sixty eight to fifty eight by the Australian Diamonds in Melbourne in their final hit out before next year's World Cup. Captain Karin Burger says the dominant season does not make them favourites for the World Cup.

Formula One. Liam Lawson will step aside from practice at this weekend's Mexican Grand Prix, with Formula Two contender Nikola Tsolov taking his car for an hour as he auditions for a 2027 drive. Lawson remains unsigned for next season despite a strong year.

Golf. The Asia Pacific Amateur begins at Te Arai, with Zack Swanwick leading the New Zealand challenge. Places at next year's Masters and the Open Championship are on offer to the leading finishers.

Boxing. Joseph Parker is set to return to the ring against Frazer Clarke, with a possible slot on the undercard of the next world heavyweight title fight.
