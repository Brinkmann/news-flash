Election uncertainty and a business confidence slump headline today's news, as the campaign enters its final fortnight.

New Zealand.

A survey of around one hundred and fifty of the country's top business executives shows widening doubt over Christopher Luxon's political future, though he scored better on his capacity to drive economic growth. Separately, fewer than half of business leaders feel positive about their own firm's performance, with expectations for improvement at a record low. Election policy uncertainty topped chief executives' concerns, scoring seven point six two out of ten. Election day is November the seventh.

A Starship Hospital nurse says staff shortages are causing dire consequences, including children missing medication and dosing errors. The Herald reports some cancer treatments have been delayed by up to seven days. The warning comes as Newsroom confirms its investigation into a Starship child abuse case has been referred by the Criminal Cases Review Commission to the Court of Appeal, after new evidence suggested medical reasons may explain a baby's brain and rib injuries.

Economists expect annual inflation to September to slow to around three point nine percent, driven by easing fuel prices through July and August. ANZ economists say New Zealand's housing bubble has mostly deflated, though prices may have a little further to fall. Lodestone Energy began trading on the NZX today after a seventy five million dollar initial public offering, the first significant listing on the exchange in about five years.

Western Bay of Plenty councillor Alan Kurtovich has confirmed his resignation over the district's Māori wards decision was the direct trigger for the byelection now under way. In Tauranga, a man who stabbed a friend to death after a party has had his sentence reduced, and may serve as little as two years for manslaughter.

Christopher Luxon toured Bay of Plenty's rural heartland, visiting Pongakawa, Edgecumbe and Whakatāne alongside East Coast candidate Dana Kirkpatrick. Trade Minister Todd McClay said rural exports reached a record sixty five point two billion dollars, supporting three hundred and sixty thousand jobs. Just over three hundred and fifty thousand New Zealanders remain unenrolled to vote, with the Electoral Commission sending teams to malls, markets and hospitals before the deadline.

World.

An Australian man, Brendan Nugent, forty three, has appeared in court charged over the car attack on independent MP Helen Haines, who has lost both legs. Deleted social media posts reveal a mental health spiral in the years before the attack, and police say they are examining a link between extreme ideology and psychological vulnerability. Locals describe Haines, who had fought for the community, as a kind and committed local member.

Russia says it is willing in principle to resume talks with Ukraine and the United States, after President Vladimir Putin met Trump's special envoy Steve Witkoff in Saint Petersburg. Doubts remain over whether this will shift the wider standoff, with Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky still at odds with Washington over its approach.

Sudan's army drones hit a funeral convoy and a fuel market, killing more than three hundred people, according to sources. Rwanda-backed M23 rebels have entered the Congolese city of Goma, with the United Nations estimating more than one million displaced people now inside the city as residents report ongoing gunfire.

Meta has been fined twenty five billion dollars over what regulators called lies about user data privacy, the largest such fine against the company to date, larger than its previous eighteen billion dollar settlement in California.

Technology.

A critical security update has been issued for PHP, a programming language used widely across the internet, after researchers described the vulnerabilities as among the most significant ever found, identified with the help of AI analysis. Google has resolved a cloud outage that disrupted UK banks and websites, with Revolut among the worst affected. Elon Musk has confirmed he is considering merging Tesla and SpaceX, with special committees at both companies now examining what would be the largest corporate merger in history.

Sport.

Rugby League World Cup. The Kiwis and Kiwi Ferns play France in Sunday's double header, with five debutants named, including Christchurch front-rower Tanner Stowers-Smith, seventy five years after his great-grandfather last wore the jersey. The second round of pool play this week will decide which Pacific teams stay in contention for top two finishes in both men's and women's competitions.

Netball. The Silver Ferns beat Australia again in Christchurch, putting them in a commanding position to win the Constellation Cup for just the fourth time. Coach Dame Noeline Taurua admitted a substitution error at the shooting end despite the win. The team travels to Australia for the final two tests.

Rugby. Black Ferns coach Whitney Hansen expects a French backlash in the second test, after a record-breaking rout in the first, and is set to make several changes. All Blacks lock Shannon Frizell is the latest player facing an injury cloud ahead of the northern tour, adding to a year of attrition through the squad.

Cricket. Australia were rolled for ninety nine runs and forced to follow on against South Africa, with a former South African great calling the batting collapse gutless. James Anderson, forty four, has not been offered a new contract by Lancashire, likely ending his playing career.

Formula One. Liam Lawson will headline VCARB garage sessions this week as part of his build-up to the United States Grand Prix in Texas.

Coming up. The Rugby League World Cup double header runs Sunday, Kiwis and Kiwi Ferns both in action. Black Ferns play France in the second test this week. The United States Grand Prix runs this weekend. Labour Day falls on Monday.
