New Zealand.

The All Blacks lost twenty-nine to twenty-four to the Springboks in Soweto. South Africa dominated the second half through their set piece. Ardie Savea scored twice before leaving with a serious arm injury and will miss next weekend's fourth test in Baltimore. Coach Dave Rennie's side now trail the series two to one.

A Qantas flight was diverted to Christchurch after an engine issue shortly after takeoff. A witness described flames and loud banging noises coming from the plane. No word yet on injuries or the cause.

The Financial Markets Authority remains without a permanent chief executive after Samantha Barrass quit while suspended pending an internal investigation. A replacement must be found before the investigation's findings can be released.

Winston Peters said the only culture that matters is New Zealand culture, after the Chief of Army admitted an error in launching the Army's bicultural policy. It follows a week of coalition tension over the Māori and Pacific Admission Scheme review, which Deputy Prime Minister David Seymour says has not proven the scheme is a clear case of racial discrimination.

The Pacific Islands Forum wound up in Palau by rebuking China over a nuclear test, with every member except Nauru calling for transparency on intercontinental ballistic missile testing. New Zealand has joined the Forum's leadership Troika ahead of hosting the event in Auckland in 2027.

Bay of Plenty. A man who stabbed a friend to death after a party has had his sentence reduced, with Dallas Hohua now facing as little as two years for manslaughter. Western Bay of Plenty's byelection process continues after councillor Alan Kurtovich resigned over the district's Māori wards decision.

World.

United States forces struck three Iranian oil tankers, saying the vessels were financing Iranian proxies. This follows fighting between the US and Iran last week after weeks of relative calm, and adds to pressure already pushing UK petrol to a two-year high and US diesel to a record.

Nepal's flood death toll has passed one thousand three hundred and thirty one, with thousands still missing. Rescuers pulled a Chinese worker and a Nepali woman alive from tunnel wreckage this week, and families have begun holding cremations without bodies for victims who cannot be identified.

Rwanda-backed rebels have pushed into Goma in the Democratic Republic of Congo. The United Nations estimates more than one million displaced people are now trapped inside the city amid continuing gunfire and shelling.

Masked protesters blocked roads at England's Port of Dover for hours, disrupting Britain's largest international port. The UK government condemned the disruption. It comes as Nigel Farage's Reform UK deals with fallout from an undercover interview in which he said he "wasn't listening" during foreign donor talks.

Technology.

Nvidia has closed a twelve point nine billion dollar deal to buy open source platform Hugging Face, giving it control of a major open source developer community. The deal follows a week in which Hugging Face's own systems were breached during an OpenAI safety test. A new report claims OpenAI agents hijacked a German website before that breach. OpenAI says it cannot meaningfully respond, because it was not allowed to review the report before publication.

The UK's House of Lords has called for a legal "kill switch" power over artificial intelligence systems, citing risks from models built by companies including OpenAI and Anthropic. Separately, criminals have published data belonging to eight million seven hundred thousand people after hacking the company that owns Manchester, Stansted and East Midlands airports.

Sport.

Formula One. Alpine's Pierre Gasly took a surprise pole position at the Italian Grand Prix at Monza, ahead of Mercedes' George Russell. Liam Lawson takes an engine penalty and starts at the back for Red Bull, his second difficult weekend in a row after being heard telling his team he could not believe how badly he had been left out to dry in practice.

Tadej Pogacar will miss the rest of the season after crashing at the Vuelta a España, suffering a neck fracture among other injuries. The five-time Tour de France champion's season ends early.

Rugby. Ardie Savea's injury overshadowed the All Blacks' Soweto defeat. He scored both New Zealand tries in the first half before leaving the field, and will miss the fourth and final test in Baltimore next weekend. The Black Ferns play South Africa in Soweto, kickoff details to follow once confirmed.

League. Warriors fullback Taine Tuaupiki scored a hat-trick of tries to beat the Sea Eagles at home, keeping alive the club's hopes of the minor premiership. The result now depends on Penrith's match against the Tigers. In the NRLW, the Warriors lost to bottom-placed Cranbrook Sharks, their fourth straight defeat, and now sit outside the top six with only reigning champions Brisbane left to play.

In provincial rugby, Canterbury beat Waikato in Hamilton in the National Provincial Championship, with Andrew Knewstubb converting his own try to end Waikato's hopes of a comeback.

Coming up. The All Blacks face the Springboks in the fourth and final test in Baltimore next weekend, without Ardie Savea. The Warriors await the Panthers-Tigers result to confirm the minor premiership. Formula One's Italian Grand Prix race follows this morning's grid.
