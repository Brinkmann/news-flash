Election special votes land this afternoon, with Newsroom and RNZ both reporting the final count will decide who forms the next government.

New Zealand.

Final election results are released this afternoon. Around four hundred thousand special votes remain uncounted, and the result is too close to call. The Opportunity Party's negotiating power with National depends heavily on these numbers. Final seat allocations could still shift which parties are needed to govern.

RNZ says it is managing an attempted cyber breach, working with specialist security providers to monitor the impact. The Public Service Association wants a wider review of RNZ's financial crisis and will file legal action next week unless the broadcaster halts plans to cut fifty eight jobs. David Seymour wants a tougher inquiry finished by Christmas. Federated Farmers has separately called proposed cuts to RNZ's rural reporting team a kick in the guts for rural communities.

Health New Zealand has rehired at least one hundred and sixty six workers made redundant since July twenty twenty two, not counting those who returned as contractors. The agency has logged one thousand, nine hundred and seven redundancy payments over that period. It raises questions about whether the original cuts were properly planned.

A new biometrics system is being sought by the immigration ministry after a fifty million dollar project failure, described internally as regretful expenditure. The replacement needs to handle ten thousand photo comparisons an hour against twenty million stored templates.

In the Bay of Plenty, Tauranga District Council says Mount Maunganui could reopen next month, after spending more than seven million dollars on its response to the fatal Mauao landslide. Reopening still needs support from iwi and a trust before it proceeds. A Tauranga man has also come forward as the winner of last week's one million dollar Lotto prize, after spotting an unchecked bonus ticket lying in his car.

World.

Nvidia shares have continued to slide as fears over an AI bubble intensify, with the Nasdaq down almost four per cent in early trading. The United States attorney general has avoided confirming whether chief executive Jensen Huang remains under investigation following his reinstatement last week. The saga has already added then erased hundreds of billions of dollars in company value, and lawmakers are still demanding answers about Donald Trump's role in bringing Huang back.

The United States has announced new tariffs on a host of trading partners, including China and the European Union. The move raises the risk of fresh trade retaliation from major economies already under pressure from slowing growth.

Thirty one people have been killed and twenty one injured after a fuel tanker crashed and caught fire on a busy ring road in Cairo. It is the latest in a series of transport disasters in Egypt.

Israeli judges have recommended dropping the most serious corruption charges against Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, saying there is little chance of conviction in one of three cases against him. Netanyahu says the trial should now end. Separately, an Israeli strike in Gaza City has killed a senior Hamas security official along with his wife and two teenage daughters.

Technology.

Nvidia's turmoil continues to dominate technology coverage, with San Francisco's AI industry increasingly worried about a sector overheating after the rapid share price swings tied to Jensen Huang's exit and return. The United States has moved to blacklist Asian firm Megaspeed over allegations it funnelled AI chips to China, a case that surfaced after a former executive's affair with Huang became public.

The United States has approved a SpaceX plan to launch up to a million data satellites, aimed at meeting growing demand for AI computing power. The scale of the project is unprecedented for low orbit infrastructure, and regulators will monitor congestion and interference risks as launches begin.

Sport.

Rugby. All Black wing Will Jordan has gone home injured ahead of Saturday's rematch with England at Twickenham, and captain Codie Taylor is in doubt. Midfielder Jordie Barrett says players and coaches must demand better after a difficult season. Beauden Barrett has reportedly signed with Bristol Bears, joining after the twenty twenty seven World Cup. Wallace Sititi is rated the bigger loss from the All Blacks' exodus of players heading overseas. World Rugby has suspended Oceania Rugby from its council pending a governance investigation, with Fiji questioning the regional body's relevance.

Football, Champions League. Liverpool beat Club Brugge, Alexander Isak scoring twice to take his season tally to ten. Manchester United drew with Sporting after a goalkeeping error from Senne Lammens denied them victory. Barcelona beat Sabah four one, Anthony Gordon scoring twice. Lille beat Bayern Munich, helped by an own goal, ending Bayern's unbeaten run this campaign. Borussia Dortmund fought back to draw with Arsenal.

Cricket. The Black Caps continue preparations for the second test against India, after holding on with their last five wickets to save the first match. Paceman Jacob Duffy is expected to again bowl heavy overs in Christchurch.

Boxing. Joseph Parker will fight WBO heavyweight champion Daniel Dubois in his comeback bout, his first world title shot since twenty seventeen.

No Formula One or Warriors fixtures to report today.
