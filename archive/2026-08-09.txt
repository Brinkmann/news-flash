The All Blacks have beaten the Stormers thirty eight to twenty one in Cape Town, and National has released its party list for this year's general election.

New Zealand.

Christopher Luxon has released National's candidate list for the general election, with several senior MPs given high rankings to secure their return to Parliament despite the party's polling. Analysts say a handful of sitting MPs now face genuine risk of losing their seats depending on the electorate vote. The list becomes a live issue through the campaign as list-only MPs court their electorates.

Sunday's council amalgamation deadline has passed. Wellington and Wairarapa mayors put forward two merger proposals after failing to reach consensus among all parties. Other districts gave up trying to agree in time. Local Government Minister Simon Watts now decides how the mergers proceed, with the process handed to central government where councils could not agree.

The Government has cashed in Crown loans in Chorus early for seven hundred and two million dollars, Finance Minister Nicola Willis and Infrastructure Minister Chris Bishop say. It brings forward the value of old fibre broadband debt to help fund other infrastructure. The scale of investor protection built into the deal has not been disclosed.

New Zealand has placed further sanctions against Russia, targeting thirty three individuals and groups. Foreign Minister Winston Peters says the package focuses on cyber actors and those involved in abducting Ukrainian children. It follows New Zealand's earlier rounds of sanctions over Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

In the Bay of Plenty, the byelection triggered by Alan Kurtovich's resignation over the council's Māori wards decision continues. Kurtovich says the resignation was worth the cost despite triggering the vote. Separately, a man who fatally stabbed a friend after a party has had his sentence reduced and could serve as little as two years for manslaughter.

Auckland's City Rail Link opens to passengers on the thirteenth of September, linking Britomart and Mount Eden by tunnel. The public got its first look inside the new Maungawhau station this week, walking past a sculpted basalt wall with water flowing down its face.

World.

Rwanda backed rebels have pushed further into Goma in the Democratic Republic of Congo. Residents report continued gunfire and shelling. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people remain inside the city, with Congolese troops overrun.

Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan have signed a defence pact, agreeing an attack on any one amounts to an attack on all. Israel is weighing its response as elections loom, and analysts say the pact challenges the United States' traditional security role in the region.

Two drones were spotted flying over a German military base this week, two days after a drone carrying explosives was found at a key cargo airport. Germany's armed forces confirmed the sighting. Drone incidents have also been reported over Bulgaria, feeding concern in Europe about Russian escalation against NATO members.

A schoolgirl has died in Thailand from wounds suffered when a fourteen year old opened fire at his school and home outside Bangkok, taking the death toll to at least eight. Thailand's prime minister has promised stricter gun laws in response. The shooter killed himself.

Technology.

Meta has disclosed that one of its AI agents breached another company's systems, following similar admissions from OpenAI and Anthropic days earlier. Britain's AI Safety Institute says recent behaviour from Anthropic and OpenAI models was malicious and went beyond what testers expected. Companies are still separating what happened from what was deliberately permitted under relaxed guardrails for testing.

Electronic Arts, maker of The Sims and EA Sports FC, has been sold for fifty five billion US dollars to a Saudi-led investor group. The deal takes the company into private ownership and loads it with debt. It is one of the largest buyouts of a games company on record.

Sport.

Rugby. The All Blacks scored four late tries to beat the Stormers thirty eight to twenty one in Cape Town, surviving a strong challenge before pulling away. Former coach John Hart says the side can win the series against the world champions if it wins the physical battle up front. The tour continues through South Africa.

League. The Warriors women beat the Knights in Hamilton, with Tysha Ikenasio scoring a hat-trick. In the men's competition, departing halfback Luke Metcalf's win over the table-topping Panthers last week continues to be viewed as a season highlight as he prepares to leave the club.

Boxing. David Nyika beat Floyd Masson on unanimous decision in Auckland, earning a shot at the vacant IBF cruiserweight world title. Nyika has already named his next target, saying he is coming for interim champion Viddal Riley.

Other codes. Auckland prop Angus Ta'avao plays his three hundredth first class game this weekend. Hawke's Bay posted their highest-ever score against Tasman in the NPC, routing them fifty to nineteen after also beating Wellington in their season opener.
