Warren Buffett has stepped down as chairman of Berkshire Hathaway after six decades, the lead story today.

New Zealand.

An inquiry has found Oranga Tamariki missed opportunities to keep the Phillips children safe, RNZ reports. The children's father was described in the inquiry as capable and loving. The agency's chief executive says she is seeking answers. Petrol has hit three dollars twenty five a litre, and the Automobile Association warns prices will likely stay high until mid November, with both RNZ and the Herald reporting the warning. An inquest has heard psychiatric patient Zakariye Mohamed Hussein was assessed as high risk of violence two weeks before he committed murder, though he was rated low risk to the community. Bootcamps legislation has passed its final reading under urgency and will become law, with opposition parties promising to scrap it if they win the election. Auckland CBD height limits could be scrapped under a government and council proposal to capitalise on the new City Rail Link, though a major Auckland developer says it doubts any new apartments would be built in the short term as a result. In the Bay of Plenty, Western Bay of Plenty's Māori wards decision triggered councillor Alan Kurtovich's resignation and the byelection that follows, confirmed by Kurtovich himself. Dallas Hohua, who stabbed a friend to death after a party, has had his sentence reduced and may serve as little as two years for manslaughter, a court record.

World.

Berkshire Hathaway's Warren Buffett has stepped down as chairman after leading the company since nineteen sixty five, turning a struggling textile mill into a global conglomerate, both the BBC and Al Jazeera report. His son Howard becomes chairman, with Buffett taking the title of chairman emeritus immediately. French President Emmanuel Macron says Russian hybrid attacks against Europe are intensifying, and has tasked his government with protecting critical infrastructure and defence sites, according to the BBC. Spanish police have moved thousands of migrants off beaches in Ceuta, using batons and firing shots into the air, after around ten thousand of the seventy two thousand who crossed from Morocco in July remained in the enclave, reported by both the BBC and Al Jazeera. UEFA and CONCACAF are demanding FIFA pay ten million US dollars to each of its two hundred and eleven member federations from World Cup reserves, escalating their push to remove president Gianni Infantino, corroborated by BBC Sport and Al Jazeera.

Technology.

King Charles's warning this week that artificial intelligence poses an existential danger if it falls into the wrong hands continues to draw responses. Microsoft AI head Mustafa Suleyman has repeated his concern that uncontrolled AI could produce a rival silicon species, telling the BBC he believes Anthropic is in effect teaching its Claude model that it may be conscious. Separately, Snapchat's chief executive Evan Spiegel told the BBC he is open to time limits for teenage users, calling it an important step for the industry. No date has been set for any change.

Sport.

League. York Valkyrie beat Leeds Rhinos to reach the Super League Grand Final, a matter of record from BBC Sport. In the NRL, the Warriors' run to the finals continues this week following their elimination final win, which aired yesterday.

Cricket. England beat Sri Lanka by six wickets in Cardiff to win the T20 series with a match to spare. Jos Buttler passed fifteen thousand career T20 runs in the innings, the first batsman to reach that mark, a matter of record confirmed by both RNZ and BBC Sport.

Football. Cristiano Ronaldo, aged forty one, has been named in Portugal's first squad under new coach Jorge Jesus since the World Cup, for upcoming Nations League fixtures, reported by the BBC. England manager Thomas Tuchel has recalled Trent Alexander-Arnold and Cole Palmer for his first squad since the World Cup, also from the BBC.

Netball. Dame Noeline Taurua's reappointment as Silver Ferns coach through to the twenty twenty seven World Cup aired yesterday and stands, following her side's gold medal in Glasgow.

Golf. Ryan Fox remains occupied with the aftermath of his major win, with no time to focus on anything else, RNZ reports. His form at Wentworth aired yesterday.

Basketball. The Breakers open their home NBL season against the Illawarra Hawks, aiming for a win after an injury setback in the pre-season, RNZ reports.

Rugby. All Blacks coach Dave Rennie faces selection questions ahead of the World Cup, including who deputises for halfback Cam Roigard, Stuff reports. Black Ferns coach Whitney Hansen has acknowledged a shortage of options at first five eighth, calling it a struggle following an issue that hurt the team at last year's World Cup.

Cycling. Finn Fisher-Black's criticism of Cycling New Zealand's funding policy for the Road World Championships, warning it could push riders toward their own ambitions over the national team, aired yesterday and continues to draw comment.
