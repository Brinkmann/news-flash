The Reserve Bank raises the Official Cash Rate today despite earlier signalling a pause, and the election race tightens further with ten days to go.

New Zealand.

The Reserve Bank is expected to raise the Official Cash Rate by twenty five basis points to three point zero percent today, the third consecutive rise and the highest level in a year. It had previously signalled a pause, and the move lands days before the election, which commentators call rare. Home loan rates are expected to move in response.

A new poll shows the gap between National and Labour narrowing further, with Opportunity remaining the power broker between the two blocs. The Electoral Commission has rejected claims of voter suppression after reports tens of thousands could be turned away from voting. Winston Peters is defending a New Zealand First advertisement blaming the sinking of navy ship Manawanui on diversity hiring, saying the commander "was not qualified." Candidate Ron Mark denies the ad has anything to do with the vessel being captained by a woman. Christopher Luxon has rejected claims New Zealand is more divided.

Police are investigating a potential breakthrough in the Katrina Jefferies cold case. A newly surfaced report shows police had earlier recommended a suspect be charged with her murder. The case is being reassessed with new information and old exhibits under review.

In Western Bay of Plenty, Alan Kurtovich has confirmed his council resignation was driven by the district's Māori wards decision, triggering the current byelection. In Kawerau, a man convicted of manslaughter after stabbing a friend following a party has had his sentence reduced and may serve as little as two years.

A woman shot in the leg at a Mount Wellington property in Auckland is in stable condition. Two men detained after the Tuesday night incident have been released without charge. Separately, a pilot escaped injury after his aircraft's propeller broke mid flight at twelve hundred feet above Matakana Island in the Bay of Plenty, forcing an emergency landing.

World.

Israel's election has produced no clear winner, according to exit polls, with the Eisenkot bloc reported ahead of Benjamin Netanyahu's camp. Official results are due November the fourth. Turnout was described as among the highest in the country's history.

Rwanda backed rebels have entered Goma, a safe haven city in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with residents reporting gunfire and shelling after Congolese troops were overrun. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city.

NATO has mounted its first coordinated response to suspected sabotage of undersea cables, after another cable was severed in the Baltic Sea. The alliance treats it as part of a pattern against critical infrastructure.

Wildfires and toxic haze continue across Southeast Asia, with rain in Indonesia offering brief relief, though underground peat fires are still burning.

Technology.

Anthropic's initial public offering continues to draw scrutiny, a day after its own prospectus warned of catastrophic risk from artificial intelligence. New Zealand retail investors cannot take part directly in the float but will be able to buy shares once trading begins on Nasdaq. Australian investors have direct access.

The global scramble over a critical flaw in the PHP programming language continues, now several days in. Artificial intelligence tools helped uncover the vulnerability. The UK's AI minister says five hundred new vulnerabilities have been found in government systems. The large scale cyber event authorities feared has still not materialised.

Sport.

Rugby League World Cup. The Kiwis make five changes for a must win pool game against Fiji on the Gold Coast on Saturday, recalling several frontline players.

Rugby. Damian McKenzie and Beauden Barrett are set to combine again for the All Blacks against Scotland on the northern tour. Coach Dave Rennie says depth in the key positions is now "a luxury" following injuries to Love and Mo'unga. Leicester Fainga'anuku remains named as a loose forward in the squad.

Netball. Silver Fern Maddy Gordon says she had "had enough" of a boisterous Australian crowd during the third Test in Sydney, after mocking the booing from the stands.

Golf. Ryan Fox finished in the top five on the DP World Tour event in Korea, three shots behind the Japanese winner. Dame Lydia Ko has denied social media claims she gave private golf lessons to South Korea's first lady after being drawn into a political controversy there.

Boxing. Joseph Parker is set to return to the ring against Frazer Clarke, with a possible undercard slot on the next world heavyweight title fight.

Basketball. The Breakers lost eighty six to eighty four to the Taipans in Cairns, a matter of record, and sit four wins and five losses on the season.

Rugby, domestic. Canterbury have defended their National Provincial Championship title, beating Northland after trailing twenty one to ten at halftime, with five second half tries ending Northland's unbeaten run.
