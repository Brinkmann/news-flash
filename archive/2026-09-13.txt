New Zealand.

Police have charged a Tauranga man over what they called a vandalism spree, after political election hoardings were damaged in Papamoa. Charges have been laid, with the case now before the courts. It comes as candidates across the country ramp up local campaigning ahead of the election.

Auckland's City Rail Link ran its first full day of scheduled passenger services yesterday, a day after officially opening. Crowds of train enthusiasts turned out to ride the new tunnels between Waitematā and Te Waihorotiu stations. Timetables and journey times change across the wider network, though fares stay the same. Former Auckland mayor Len Brown said the project would not have been possible without the amalgamation into the single super city council.

Louise Upston has fronted publicly on the Ministry of Social Development's payment failures, telling RNZ she accepts responsibility, calling the errors incredibly frustrating and unacceptable. The ministry's review found tens of thousands of payments were wrongly suspended after a law change. Affected beneficiaries say they want assurance the errors will not recur.

Labour's Willie Jackson was challenged over the party's capital gains tax policy, struggling to explain details before saying he needed to get back to a colleague. Leader Chris Hipkins said Jackson should have been clearer. It follows a week of immigration policy clashes, with ACT and New Zealand First pushing tighter controls and Phil Twyford calling the wider debate a dumpster fire, as National, Labour and the Greens warn against populist rhetoric.

One person has died and another was taken to Waikato Hospital by helicopter in moderate condition after a house fire on Matakana Island in the Bay of Plenty. Fire and Emergency and police attended. Nearly twelve and a half thousand wild kiwifruit vines have been destroyed across a thousand Bay of Plenty properties, as authorities continue to contain a known vine infestation.

Auckland's Northwestern motorway had a section closed after a crash, causing delays for weekend travellers. Wellington Airport was evacuated on Saturday afternoon after burnt cooking triggered a smoke alarm, with two Fire and Emergency crews responding before it reopened.

World.

Saudi Arabia has shut a key oil pipeline after a drone attack launched from Iraq, with Baghdad removing a military commander and opening an investigation into the strike, which came from an area bordering Iran. Oil prices have jumped amid signs the Houthi and wider regional conflict will not resolve quickly. Houthi forces say they struck Saudi Aramco facilities directly, extending a pattern of attacks across the region.

French officials are investigating whether a deliberate act caused a passenger train derailment on Friday that injured forty four people, after a rail fragment was found on the tracks. The findings of that investigation are still to come.

The death toll from a ferry fire in the Philippines has risen to seventy six, with more people still missing. Access to the burnt-out vessel has been slowed by toxic fumes and lingering heat, the coast guard says.

Donald Trump was welcomed to Ireland with a satirical trash-covered double appearing ahead of him in Dublin. During his visit, Trump told Irish leader Micheál Martin that a united Ireland would be fantastic, prompting Downing Street to say Britain will stick to the terms of the Good Friday Agreement.

Technology.

Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei has called for artificial intelligence development to slow down, citing growing concern that AI models may become capable of inflicting serious worldwide damage. It follows the company's threat intelligence report detailing a blocked attempt to use its models to assist biological weapons development, and a former senior Anthropic researcher's warning that AI carries more than a ten per cent chance of catastrophic harm to humanity. The warnings have met scepticism from some Silicon Valley executives and investors, the BBC reports. The United Kingdom's Cabinet Office has separately rejected calls for a mandatory AI kill switch, saying the technology cannot simply be turned off.

Sport.

League. The Warriors were beaten by the Dolphins in their NRL qualifying final, and now face the Newcastle Knights in an elimination semifinal in Auckland. Fullback Taine Tuaupiki had said the side was focused on the basics heading in. Attention now turns fully to the sudden-death semifinal.

Rugby. The Ranfurly Shield stays in Whangārei after Northland thrashed Waikato thirty four to seventeen, defending the log o' wood in a dominant performance. The All Blacks play South Africa in Baltimore on Sunday morning, the final test of the rivalry series, with coach Dave Rennie making changes and saying every player selected fully deserves the jersey. The Black Ferns beat France in their WXV Global Series opener in Lyon.

Formula One. Lando Norris took pole position for the Spanish Grand Prix in Madrid, beating Kimi Antonelli and Max Verstappen with what he called a gorgeous lap. Liam Lawson qualified eighth, his third straight top ten session of the weekend. The race is a first Formula One event in Madrid in more than four decades.

Cricket. England beat Pakistan by eight wickets in the third Test at Edgbaston, completing a three-nil series sweep. Captain Joe Root and Jordan Cox guided the chase of one hundred and thirty after an early scare. Root said England are in a better place than at the start of the summer.

Coming up. The Warriors face Newcastle in an NRL elimination semifinal this week. The All Blacks played South Africa in Baltimore on Sunday morning. The Vuelta a España and Solheim Cup both approach their final stages this week.
