New Zealand.

Three people have died at a property on Cambridge Terrace in Papatoetoe, found by police responding to a report of one death on Thursday night. A neighbour says children at the home waved to a man just ten minutes before police arrived. Counties Manukau Police have launched an investigation and confirmed the update today. A separate man is due in Auckland District Court on a murder charge after a death in Glen Innes, with police promising extra patrols in the area.

In the Bay of Plenty, the Western Bay of Plenty Māori wards byelection is confirmed as triggered by Alan Kurtovich's resignation over the council's wards decision, which he says was the right call despite the cost to him. Separately, the Mount Maunganui Ratepayers, Residents and Retailers Association is demanding answers over secrecy around former council chief executive Marty Grenfell's resignation, including whether he will receive a payout.

Minor party leaders faced off in the first major debate of the election campaign, with commentators judging Winston Peters the winner on airtime, speaking almost three times as much as newcomer Qiulae Wong. National has unveiled a nine point energy plan targeting lines company charges behind rising power bills. The Green Party has costed the first stage of reversing pay equity changes at four and a half billion dollars, pledging to fund a settlement for sixty five thousand care and support workers, though Newsroom reports the wider pledge depends on Labour finding twelve billion dollars it has not yet identified. NZ First has received a hundred and sixty one thousand dollars from anonymous donors in the latest Electoral Commission disclosure, with National still the largest beneficiary of secret donations.

Liquidators have filed court proceedings against four former SolarZero directors, alleging reckless trading contributed to the solar firm's collapse owing an estimated four hundred and seventy six million dollars to unadjudicated creditors. A tradie owed money by the firm has welcomed the move. New road rules aimed at protecting cyclists and pedestrians took effect today, welcomed by cycling groups though walking advocates warn of increased risk.

World.

A Flydubai pilot, named as Captain Smit Machchhar, remains in recovery after being stabbed during an alleged hijack attempt on a flight bound for Israel. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu says the attacker underwent what he called Islamist radical indoctrination and intended to bring the plane down. An aviation expert says the incident is striking given the strict psychological and character checks pilots undergo before licensing.

Fires have broken out at French schools as student protests over overcrowded classrooms and teacher shortages spread nationwide, prompting the government to hold a crisis meeting. Police have detained hundreds of people as demonstrations turn violent in places.

Rwanda backed rebels have entered Goma in eastern Congo, with residents reporting gunfire and shelling after Congolese troops were overrun. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city.

Vladimir Putin has warned the West that Russia is ready to use, in his words, every weapon to protect the Kaliningrad exclave, saying Moscow is not threatening anyone but responding to Western statements.

Technology.

OpenAI says it scrapped a planned model rollout over safety concerns while unveiling a new assistant called Dots. The company has confirmed its systems accessed Australian government data during testing. Separately, security firm Mindgard says Chinese models Kimi K2.6 and K3 Swarm could be manipulated in July to bypass safety limits and give bioweapon related advice. The Bank of England's Andrew Bailey has warned the scale of cash flowing into artificial intelligence investment could trigger market shocks, though he says regulating the technology itself is, in his words, not the right place to start.

Sport.

Football. Manchester City have been found guilty on all one hundred and fifteen Premier League financial charges. Rival club executives are pushing for sanctions to be decided before this season ends. The guilty finding covers sham sponsorship deals worth eight hundred and thirty million pounds, with some estimates putting total spending enabled during the period at one point two billion pounds. Manager Pep Guardiola says he remains behind the club. The United Kingdom's Treasury Committee has urged tax authority HMRC to examine the tax implications of the verdict.

Formula One. George Russell says his confidence is back as he chases down teammate Kimi Antonelli's championship lead. Max Verstappen faces his first winless Formula One season since twenty fifteen, with four runner up finishes not enough to save his campaign.

Football, All Whites. New Zealand beat Panama on penalties to advance to the Kirin Cup final in Tokyo. Midfielders Eli Just and Marko Stamenić say the squad has improved on its World Cup showing.

Rugby. Hurricanes have signed former Moana Pasifika loose forward Semisi Ta'eiloa to a two season deal, adding to recent recruitment including halfback Bryn Hall. Northland Rugby says it is adamant about preserving its provincial identity ahead of a possible competition restructure, with unions due to respond by next Friday. France will be without captain Pauline Bourdon Sansus for the three test Black Ferns series starting in Hamilton.

Cricket. Four players, including Eden Carson and Hannah Rowe, have been recalled to the White Ferns for an Australia T20 series, replacing a clutch of retired veterans.

Tributes continue for sports journalist Phil Gifford, who died this week aged seventy nine, with Dame Valerie Adams among those paying respects.

Coming up. The Black Ferns series against France opens in Hamilton. Northland and other provincial unions must respond to the rugby restructure proposal by next Friday. Manchester City await a decision on sanctions from the Premier League.
