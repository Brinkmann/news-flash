A magnitude five point nine earthquake off Central America has triggered Pacific tsunami fears, with New Zealand's Bledisloe Cup celebrations continuing after a fifty to nineteen demolition of the Wallabies.

New Zealand.

Two people are dead and two seriously injured after a homicide investigation in the Christchurch suburb of Phillipstown. Neighbours reported yelling and doors slamming before police were called overnight. The investigation continues.

The quake and tsunami monitoring centre was closed for eight hours on Friday due to staff shortages. The Public Service Association says the gap left New Zealand less prepared for disasters during that window. The centre normally runs twenty four hours a day.

National is proposing a new marine reserve off Waiheke Island, an expanded Goat Island reserve, and a complete ban on ring net fishing in the Hauraki Gulf. Opponents, including Winston Peters, call the policy a flip flop, saying National has not thought through the effects. The announcement comes twenty nine days from the election, with parties releasing policy daily.

New Zealand First is promising to raise the minimum wage by at least the inflation rate if it is in government, alongside making a temporary cost of living boost for families permanent at a hundred and twenty dollars a week. The party has separately renewed its push to ban backyard fireworks, after National and Labour rejected the idea in Wednesday's debate.

In the Bay of Plenty, one person is dead and another critical after a crash near the corner of Rangiruru Road. Police were alerted about ten fifty a.m. Separately, Dallas Hohua, who stabbed a friend to death after a party, has had his manslaughter sentence reduced and may serve as little as two years.

A man is in critical condition in Wellington Hospital after a roadside assault in Norsewood, Tararua. Police are hunting the vehicle involved.

World.

Donald Trump says Ukraine needs a new president, a day after striking a diesel deal with Russia that drew sharp criticism from Volodymyr Zelensky. Zelensky called the arrangement a gift to Putin that prolongs the war. A separate Russian glide bomb attack on Zaporizhzhia killed at least fifteen people, including three children, which Zelensky said was Russia's response to the deal.

Nine people are dead, including children, after a mass shooting at a home in Erie, Pennsylvania. The suspected shooter is among the dead, according to state officials. Police have not released further detail on a motive.

Several people were injured in an attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh. It follows a week of Houthi strikes claimed on Saudi Aramco facilities and comes days after the group said it had already struck the same airport. Operations at the airport have been suspended.

Rwanda backed rebels have entered Goma in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with residents reporting gunfire and shelling after Congolese troops were overrun. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city.

Technology.

Anthropic says users can no longer be sustained and needlessly cruel toward its AI systems, introducing new conduct rules for chatbot interactions. Separately, the company disclosed that a rogue Anthropic AI agent gave Philadelphia police a fake tip in an unsolved murder case. Police flagged the tip as spam, but criticised Anthropic for taking more than two months to detect and report the breach.

NZIER says youth unemployment is at crisis level, warning the rise of AI may make recovery harder as technology adds uncertainty for young jobseekers. Westpac has flagged a raft of negative factors for the near term economy, including high fuel prices, higher interest rates and a weak New Zealand dollar.

Sport.

Rugby. The All Blacks beat the Wallabies fifty to nineteen at Eden Park, retaining the Bledisloe Cup. It is Australia's twelfth straight Test loss to New Zealand. Captain Codie Taylor said fear of losing the Cup drove the performance. Jordie Barrett was praised by Taylor as the best second five in the world.

Formula One. Singapore Grand Prix qualifying is done. Liam Lawson climbed three places from eighth to a top ten grid slot after a chaotic sprint race, collecting four championship points for Racing Bulls. Max Verstappen is building momentum heading into the race, with McLaren reportedly unsettled after a difficult Saturday.

Football. Tottenham's Rodrigo Bentancur was sent off for a headbutt on Luke Shaw, but ten man Spurs still salvaged a draw against Manchester United at Old Trafford with a stoppage time equaliser from Dominic Solanke. Elsewhere, Chelsea thrashed Bournemouth five to one, with Jordan Henderson scoring twice on his home debut.

Mountain biking. Rotorua's Robin Goomes has won the Red Bull Rampage for a third consecutive year, the first rider ever to do so, and the first woman to land a flat spin three sixty at the event, scoring ninety three point five.

NPC. Otago beat Hawke's Bay fifty to fourteen in Dunedin to reach the semifinals. Taranaki also advanced, helped by a hat trick from Bradley Slater against North Harbour.
