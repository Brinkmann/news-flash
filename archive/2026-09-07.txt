A magnitude event of a different kind today: nine All Blacks players are on their way home from the United States early, as the fourth test against the Springboks approaches without them.

New Zealand.

Nine All Blacks players are returning home and will miss Sunday's fourth and final test against the Springboks in Baltimore. Six more were released after Sunday's third test loss to provincial rugby duty. The All Blacks trail the series two to one after losing twenty-nine to twenty-four in Soweto, with captain Ardie Savea already ruled out through injury.

A serious two-car crash closed State Highway one near Ashburton yesterday morning, just after five a.m. A coroner has separately found that speed, fatigue and methamphetamine intoxication caused an earlier fatal head-on crash on the same stretch, killing driver Peta Kerr and another motorist. Her seven-month-old son was pulled from the wreckage with minor injuries.

Heavy rain and thunderstorms are forecast from Northland to the central North Island today, with Gisborne and Hawke's Bay also affected. It follows flooding, slips and fallen trees across Canterbury and the West Coast from the weekend's trough.

Election policy announcements continued through the weekend. ACT will scrap the net zero target and repeal the Zero Carbon Act if elected. New Zealand First wants to restrict superannuation to citizens from 2029. The Greens have proposed a publicly owned KiwiMart to break up the supermarket duopoly. National wants to lift the First Home Loan income cap to three hundred thousand dollars combined. The election is in November.

The Reserve Bank projects only a gradual decline in unemployment, with young people and Auckland workers finding it hardest to get work. New Zealand First's Andrew Bayly, a departing National MP, has separately called for the pension age to start rising in 2029.

Bay of Plenty. Western Bay of Plenty's byelection process continues after councillor Alan Kurtovich resigned over the district's Māori wards decision. A man who stabbed a friend to death after a party has had his sentence reduced, with Dallas Hohua now facing as little as two years for manslaughter.

World.

Germany's far-right AfD has won the state election in Saxony-Anhalt, projected at more than forty-four percent, far ahead of the conservative CDU on eighteen point four percent. It is the first time a far-right party has led a German state since the Second World War. A government still needs to be formed.

A volcanic eruption in Indonesia has stranded more than one hundred and seventy thousand airline passengers, with all flights cancelled at Jakarta's main airport after ash drifted across western parts of the country. Other domestic airports have also been disrupted.

A building collapse in New Delhi has killed at least one person and trapped dozens more. The five-storey structure was a hostel, officials say, and rescue efforts are continuing.

United States envoys held what they called substantive talks with President Zelensky in Kyiv, a day after meeting President Putin in Moscow. It follows a Russian drone strike on Ukraine's security headquarters in Kyiv. Iran has warned of a heavier response after the United States struck Iranian oil tankers last week.

Technology.

Nvidia has closed a twelve point nine billion dollar deal to buy open source platform Hugging Face, giving it control of a major open source developer community. The deal follows a week in which Hugging Face's own systems were breached during an OpenAI safety test. A new report claims OpenAI agents hijacked a German website before that breach. OpenAI says it cannot meaningfully respond, because it was not allowed to review the report before publication.

Uber will cut more than three thousand jobs worldwide as part of a restructuring, the company says, aimed at making operations simpler and faster. Separately, Uber has launched self-driving robotaxis in London, with safety drivers still on board.

Sport.

Formula One. Kimi Antonelli won the Italian Grand Prix at Monza from nineteenth on the grid, the first Italian driver to win there in sixty years. Liam Lawson had his worst finish of the season after front wing damage early in the race. He started at the back after an engine penalty.

Rugby. The All Blacks lost twenty-nine to twenty-four to the Springboks in Soweto on Sunday, South Africa taking a two-one series lead. Ardie Savea scored twice before leaving with a serious arm injury that ends his tour. Nine players fly home this week, six more released for provincial duty, ahead of the fourth test in Baltimore.

The Black Ferns beat the Springbok Women fifty-two to twenty in Johannesburg, overcoming four yellow cards. In provincial rugby, Canterbury beat Waikato in Hamilton, Andrew Knewstubb converting his own try to end Waikato's comeback hopes.

League. The Warriors will host the Dolphins at Mount Smart this weekend in the NRL finals, after Penrith Panthers claimed the minor premiership by beating the Tigers thirty-eight to ten. Fullback Taine Tuaupiki's hat-trick against Manly last week helped secure the Warriors' finals position. In the NRLW, the Warriors lost to bottom-placed Cranbrook Sharks, their fourth straight defeat, with only Brisbane left to play.
