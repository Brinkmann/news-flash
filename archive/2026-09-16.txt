The government has apologised to Tom Phillips' children after a damning inquiry found police and Oranga Tamariki repeatedly missed chances to find them.

New Zealand.

A public inquiry into the disappearance of the Phillips children has found concerns were minimised or overlooked by agencies for significant periods. A woman told police she had seen Tom Phillips in her car in May 2023, but officers were diverted and the sighting was not followed up. The inquiry says there might have been a real prospect of locating the children as a result. Police Commissioner Richard Chambers has accepted the findings and recommendations. Deputy Commissioner Jill Rogers says she remains proud of investigators' work. Government agencies have apologised to the children, calling the report harrowing reading. The Commissioner has yet to meet the family.

A new poll shows Labour and National both below thirty per cent support, less than two months from the November the seventh election. Newsroom analysts say the result raises the prospect of a hung parliament. Parliament has two sitting weeks left and is working through a further three weeks of legislation using urgency.

Riders from Hawke's Bay iwi Ngāti Kahungunu have delivered a fifty thousand strong petition to Parliament calling for a halt to government amendments to Treaty provisions. They travelled from Waimārama to present it.

Cue Clothing, parent company of the Cue and Veronika Maine womenswear brands, has entered receivership after nearly sixty years trading. New Zealand staff at both chains face uncertainty over their jobs. The collapse follows the failure of the brands' Australian parent company.

New Zealand's housing market has slowed sharply. Auckland's median time to sell reached fifty four days, against a ten year average of forty three. Wellington reached sixty days, against an average of forty one.

Western Bay of Plenty will hold a byelection after councillor Alan Kurtovich resigned over the council's Māori wards decision. Kurtovich says the resignation was the right call despite the cost of a byelection.

World.

NATO shot down a drone that entered Lithuanian airspace shortly after midnight, believed launched from Belarus or Russia. Denmark separately accused Russia of reckless behaviour after a Russian warship fired flares at a Danish military helicopter. NATO has also mounted its first coordinated response to suspected sabotage of an undersea cable in the Baltic Sea, after a similar cable cut in the Netherlands disrupted rail signalling on Tuesday.

Two sailors are missing after a tanker was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz. Oman says the vessel, named El Gaia, caught fire. Iran says it hit mines. The United States says it was struck by a missile and then a drone. It follows a week of wider Iran and United States tension in the region.

Rwanda backed rebels remain inside Goma in eastern Congo, a designated safe haven city, after overrunning Congolese troops. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now trapped inside the city, with aid access a growing concern.

Palestinians continue to move back into northern Gaza on foot after Israel opened a crossing through the military zone bisecting the enclave. Hamas has released four female Israeli hostages under the ceasefire deal. Freed hostages still had shrapnel in their bodies from the October the seventh attack, according to an Israeli medical official, with some held alone for extended periods.

Technology.

Anthropic says it has blocked an attempt to use its AI systems to help develop biological weapons, detailed in the company's latest threat intelligence report. It follows renewed warnings this week from chief executive Dario Amodei that AI development should slow down, and co-founder Jack Clark's call for mandatory AI kill switches. The United Kingdom government has rejected a kill switch requirement, saying it cannot simply turn AI off. United States President Donald Trump has dismissed the safety warnings as a hoax, pointing to competition with China. MPs and members of the House of Lords are separately calling for new law to address AI risks to human rights, saying existing legislation is not equipped to cope.

Sport.

League. The Warriors face Newcastle at Eden Park on Sunday in a sudden death elimination final. Dallin Watene-Zelezniak returns to the extended squad less than four weeks after breaking his right clavicle. Adam Pompey is free to play after escaping suspension for his shoulder charge against the Dolphins. Tickets sold out within an hour despite the switch to the bigger venue.

Football. Leeds United beat Newcastle four one at Elland Road to climb to third in the Premier League, with Dominic Calvert-Lewin starring. Referees' chief Howard Webb has admitted the video assistant referee did not even consider whether Enzo Fernandez was offside for Erling Haaland's winner in Sunday's Manchester derby.

Golf. The PGA Tour says it has no plans for a return pathway for LIV Golf players, after the breakaway series filed for bankruptcy protection.

Athletics. Nairobi has been named host of the 2029 World Athletics Championships, ahead of London's bid. Munich will host in 2031. It is the first time the championships will be held in Africa.

Cycling. Tom Pidcock and Zoe Backstedt lead the Great Britain team at the Road World Championships this week.

Netball. Former Silver Ferns shooters Aliyah Dunn and Te Paea Selby-Rickit have returned to the national squad ahead of the 2027 World Cup. Coach Dame Noeline Taurua says she is excited by the squad's depth heading into the tournament.

Coming up. The Warriors play Newcastle at Eden Park on Sunday. NRL finals continue this week to decide the Grand Final field. The Road World Championships continue through the week.
