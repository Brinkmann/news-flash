Auckland's City Rail Link officially opened, a decade late and five and a half billion dollars in the making. That leads today.

New Zealand.

Auckland's City Rail Link opened yesterday after ten years of construction and five and a half billion dollars spent. Prime Minister Christopher Luxon called it a major achievement. Fares stay the same, but timetables and journey lengths change across the network. CBD business owners say the disruption during construction was significant, and are hoping the opening finally brings back foot traffic.

The Ministry of Social Development says a review has found more than thirty thousand payments were wrongly suspended after a law change last year, split across two separate faults. Social Development Minister Louise Upston told RNZ she accepts responsibility, calling the errors incredibly frustrating and unacceptable. Affected pensioners and beneficiaries say they are not confident it will not happen again.

New Zealand First has unveiled an immigration overhaul, including a proposed referendum on capping resident visas at twenty thousand a year. Labour also released its own immigration policy on Friday, doubling the wait for permanent residence. ACT and New Zealand First are pushing tighter controls, while National, Labour and the Greens have warned against populist anti-immigration politics. Immigration advisers say major parties need a consistent approach regardless of who is in government, warning policy swings deter skilled workers.

Two New Zealand organisations, a health research company and a payroll provider, remain under investigation after separate cyberattacks. A cyber security company has separately warned many government agencies do not have adequate email security, leaving departments exposed to hackers.

Bay of Plenty. Western Bay of Plenty councillor Alan Kurtovich has quit over the council's Māori wards decision, triggering a byelection. He says the decision was, in his words, absolutely the right one despite the cost. Separately, Dallas Hohua has had his manslaughter sentence reduced over a fatal stabbing after a party, and may now serve as little as two years.

World.

The United States marked twenty five years since the September the eleventh attacks, with ceremonies in New York, at the Pentagon and in Pennsylvania. President Trump used a Pentagon speech to link the attacks to the case for the Iran war. Former prime minister Helen Clark said she sees alarming parallels between the Iraq war that followed 2001 and recent action against Iran.

The United Kingdom's House of Commons rejected a bill to legalise assisted dying, voting two hundred and eighty six to two hundred and seventy. Supporters say they will try again, but momentum has stalled for now.

Houthi forces say they struck Saudi Aramco facilities and have tightened their grip on the Red Sea shipping lane, reportedly seizing the island of Perim near the Bab al Mandab Strait. The United Nations says more than forty six thousand people have been displaced by escalating fighting in Yemen, a figure rising by the hour. Shipping operators say they remain wary of Red Sea routes despite a Houthi pledge to leave non-Saudi vessels alone.

More than twenty children have died in a school fire in Bukavu, in the rebel-held east of the Democratic Republic of Congo, after a stampede as pupils tried to flee.

Technology.

Anthropic says it disrupted an attempt to use its AI models to assist with biological weapons development, detailed in the company's latest threat intelligence report. It follows a warning from a former senior Anthropic researcher that artificial intelligence carries more than a ten per cent chance of catastrophic harm to humanity, a claim still drawing comment across the industry. The United Kingdom government has rejected calls for a mandatory AI kill switch, with the Cabinet Office saying the technology cannot simply be turned off, as regulators separately warn new laws are needed before AI is used more widely in the National Health Service.

Sport.

League. The Warriors host the Dolphins in an NRL qualifying final at Go Media Stadium tonight, kicking off at six oh five. Ticketmaster has warned resold tickets may be cancelled, as NRL rules restrict unauthorised resale. Adam Pompey says he is setting aside contract uncertainty to focus on the finals, and fullback Taine Tuaupiki says the side is doubling down on the basics.

Rugby. The All Blacks have made changes for the fourth test against the Springboks in Baltimore this weekend, with coach Dave Rennie saying everyone selected fully deserves it. The Black Ferns have lost captain Ruahei Demant ahead of their test against France, with Layla Sae and Alena Saili brought into the side for six changes in total.

Formula One. Liam Lawson posted two top ten finishes in practice on the new Madrid circuit at the Spanish Grand Prix, a strong first day for the Kiwi. George Russell has called the new circuit very high risk after Arvid Lindblad crashed in Friday practice.

Cricket. Pakistan set England a target of one hundred and thirty to win the third and final Test at Edgbaston, after debutant Razaullah scored eighty one off sixty three balls to help Pakistan post four hundred and forty nine in their second innings. England chase the total to complete a three-nil series sweep.

Cycling. Ireland's Eddie Dunbar won stage nineteen of the Vuelta a España. Spain's Enric Mas maintains the overall lead with two stages remaining.

Golf. The Solheim Cup is under way at the Bernardus course in the Netherlands. Europe and the United States are locked at four all after a tense finish to day one, Carlota Ciganda holding her nerve for a half after Lauren Coughlin narrowly missed the putt to win the US a point.

Coming up. The All Blacks play the Springboks in Baltimore this weekend. The Black Ferns face France on Sunday morning. The Solheim Cup continues in the Netherlands. The Vuelta a España finishes with two stages remaining this week.
