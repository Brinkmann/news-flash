A magnitude test of coalition unity, a record All Blacks win in Johannesburg, and hundreds still missing after flash floods on the Nepal Tibet border lead this morning.

New Zealand.

The All Blacks beat the Lions forty one to thirty five at Ellis Park, surviving a scare from the provincial side to keep a perfect run on this tour. Debutant Saula Ma'u scored on his first appearance. Coach Dave Rennie praised the side's composure despite a scrappy performance. Springbok captain Siya Kolisi has recovered from a hamstring injury and is cleared for the second test in Cape Town.

Christopher Luxon faced a public meeting in Kāpiti where residents pushed for a rates cap sooner than the government's four per cent proposal, due to take effect under legislation announced this week. His answers on council amalgamation reforms drew a cooler response. Labour has said it will support the rates cap bill for now, while continuing to disagree with National on other policy.

Consumer NZ says it is wrong that Woolworths banked a hundred and sixty three million dollar profit while spending four hundred million dollars on store refreshes. RNZ reports the retailer's New Zealand supermarkets improved financial performance over the June year on higher sales, despite a slowdown in the second half. The comments add pressure ahead of ongoing scrutiny of supermarket pricing.

The flu vaccine will be free for all children under five from next winter, adding about two hundred and sixty thousand children to those eligible. Associate Health Minister David Seymour says it strengthens New Zealand's vaccine response. The change follows Pharmac's decision and takes effect for the twenty twenty seven flu season.

Heavy rain is lashing the South Island's West Coast, with up to three hundred and fifty millimetres forecast over three days. Snow could fall to four hundred metres, making alpine roads difficult, and Canterbury's rivers are rising. Fog restrictions are also in place at Auckland Airport, though no flights have yet been affected.

In Western Bay of Plenty, Dallas Hohua, convicted of manslaughter after stabbing a friend to death at a party, has had his sentence reduced on appeal and may serve as little as two years. The case follows the byelection triggered by councillor Alan Kurtovich's resignation over the Māori wards decision.

World.

Flash floods on the Nepal Tibet border have killed more than one hundred people, with Nepali officials reporting four hundred and three people missing, including three hundred and forty one foreign nationals. Rescue teams from the army and police are searching border areas hit by surging debris. The death toll is expected to rise as search operations continue.

Rwanda backed rebels have entered Goma in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with residents reporting gunfire and shelling after Congolese troops were overrun. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city. No ceasefire or resolution has been reached.

Meta has agreed to an eighteen billion dollar settlement with United States states and a further payment topping sixteen billion dollars in a separate youth addiction case, requiring changes to Facebook and Instagram features. The deal follows a California federal court trial over claims the platforms harmed children's mental health. It marks one of the largest child safety settlements against a technology company.

Iran says it is fully prepared to counter expanded United States sanctions, which Washington has called an economic D Day. Analysts say clearing mines from the Strait of Hormuz, announced by President Trump, is unlikely on its own to make shipping safe there. Tehran says it has developed deep trade ties with other nations to withstand the pressure.

Technology.

TikTok will pay four hundred million dollars to the United States government in one of the largest child privacy settlements on record. The case stemmed from a twenty twenty four lawsuit alleging TikTok and parent company ByteDance collected vast amounts of data on children under thirteen without consent. It follows a pattern of regulators targeting platforms over young users' data.

Twitch and Amazon face legal action alleging Twitch used streamers' livestreams to train artificial intelligence systems without permission or compensation. The case adds to a run of disputes over how technology companies source training data for AI models.

Sport.

Football. Auckland FC play Al Ahli in the Intercontinental Cup in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, a historic first for a New Zealand club at this level.

Rugby. The All Blacks and Springboks meet in the second test of the Greatest Rivalry tour in Cape Town, with Kolisi passed fit to captain the hosts.

Tennis. Erin Routliffe and British partner Lloyd Glasspool lost to Serena Williams and Carlos Alcaraz in mixed doubles at the US Open, the glamour pairing proving too strong early on.

League. The Warriors have called up debutant Luke Laulilii for the clash with the Knights, replacing the injured Dallin Watene-Zelezniak. Two Warriors women's players return from pre-season injury to face St George Illawarra in the NRLW. The NRL will allow a special one off rule change letting former Rabbitohs forward Jai Arrow reach his hundredth game, following his retirement in May after a motor neurone disease diagnosis.

Netball. The exodus of New Zealand netballers offshore continues, with twelve players now gone amid uncertainty over the future of the ANZ Premiership.

Coming up. The Warriors host the Knights this weekend. Parliament continues debating the Online Safety Bill. The All Blacks and Springboks play in Cape Town in the coming days.

End of flash.
