New Zealand.

The High Court has ruled Rotorua Lakes Council broke the law in its handling of a sewerage pipe scheme near Lake Rotokākahi. The Rotokākahi Board of Control took the council to court over the plan to run a pipeline beside the Māori owned, tapu lake. The ruling follows years of opposition from the lake's owners. It is reported by both RNZ and the New Zealand Herald.

The government has appointed a facilitator to oversee forestry practices in Tairāwhiti, after telling Gisborne District Council its approach to slash and sediment management is too tough. Forestry Minister Todd McClay wrote to the council confirming the appointment of Doug Leeder. RNZ and the minister's own statement confirm the move, which follows repeated flooding linked to forestry slash in the region.

Chris Bishop has confirmed a detailed business case will proceed for an additional Waitematā Harbour crossing, despite NZTA completing a separate investment case only three months ago. The government and Auckland Council will split a one million dollar bill for a further study into a Meola Reef option. The project will now be Crown led, with a senior official reporting directly to ministers.

The Ministerial Advisory Group on Aged Care says the sector needs urgent change, warning the choice is between hard decisions now or more painful ones forced later. Associate Health Minister Casey Costello welcomed the report, saying governments have kept kicking the aged care can down the road. Funding and policy changes are expected to follow.

Port of Tauranga is nearing the end of a seven year consent battle over its Stella Passage expansion, with a draft decision now issued. In Western Bay of Plenty, Alan Kurtovich has confirmed he quit as a councillor over the district's Māori wards decision, triggering a byelection he says was worth the cost.

A young Waikato woman, Kyla Te Wano Greensill, has died of meningococcal disease. Her parents are urging people to watch for flu like symptoms in anyone who seems unusually unwell, warning the illness can progress within hours.

World.

Wall Street Journal reporting says Palestinians are streaming back into northern Gaza on foot after Israel allowed crossings through a military zone, following a deadlock over hostage releases. Hamas has released four more female Israeli hostages under the ceasefire deal, with an Israeli medical official saying some had spent eight months in tunnels and still carried shrapnel from the October attack. Talks continue on further releases.

NATO has mounted its first coordinated response to suspected sabotage of undersea cables, after another cable was severed in the Baltic Sea. The Wall Street Journal reports the alliance treated the incident as part of a wider pattern of attacks on critical infrastructure.

Rwanda backed rebels have entered Goma, a safe haven city in the Democratic Republic of Congo. Residents reported gunfire and shelling as Congolese troops were overrun. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city.

Donald Trump has threatened to bomb Oman if it gets in the way over an Iran deal, while separately confirming the United States will reduce military drills with South Korea, citing his relationship with North Korea's leader. Al Jazeera reports Iran is offering thirty thousand US dollars for captured or killed American soldiers, as the standoff over the Strait of Hormuz continues.

Technology.

Wall Street banks have committed five hundred billion US dollars to Nvidia to fund new AI data centres, the BBC reports, extending a surge in infrastructure spending as demand for computing capacity keeps outstripping supply. The money builds facilities to house and cool the chips that process AI workloads.

Amazon's Twitch platform is facing continued backlash after confirming it uses streamers' content to train generative AI under an opt out system. Big tech leaders, including Meta's Mark Zuckerberg, continue publishing lengthy public manifestos on AI, which commentators say reflects competition to shape the public narrative around the technology.

Sport.

Rugby. All Blacks prop Ollie Norris has been ruled out of the tour of South Africa with knee damage, the fourth player to fly home early through injury. Hooker Ratima is in doubt. Attack coach Mike Blair says the side see opportunities to beat the Springboks in Saturday's series opener, the world champions on their own turf.

Rugby. In the National Provincial Championship, Canterbury beat Bay of Plenty thirty three to twenty eight at Te Kaha to retain the Ranfurly Shield, a result that has already aired. Bay of Plenty lock Seuseu Naitoa Ah Kuoi's reaction to the loss was widely shared.

League. Warriors fullback Charnze Nicoll-Klokstad has been suspended for two weeks by the NRL judiciary, for a tackle that injured Broncos fullback Reece Walsh. It follows the Warriors' win over Brisbane last weekend, a result that has already aired.

Swimming. Erika Fairweather added three more medals at the Pan Pacific Championships in California, continuing a strong meet for the New Zealand team, Newsroom reports. Her earlier bronze in that meet has already aired.

Equestrian. New Zealand's eventing team has booked a place at the Los Angeles twenty twenty eight Olympics, finishing sixth in the teams competition at the world championships in Germany.

Motorsport. Mitch Evans narrowly missed the Formula E championship title, settling for another series podium finish as he ends a decade long stint driving for Jaguar.

Netball. Netball New Zealand is facing a player exodus amid uncertainty over the domestic league's future, with the scale of the talent drain expected to become clear soon.
