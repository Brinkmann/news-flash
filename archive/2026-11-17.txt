New Zealand.

Stats NZ figures show petrol prices rose nearly twelve per cent in October on the month before, with diesel up eleven per cent. Over the year, diesel is up seventy one per cent and domestic airfares thirty three and a half per cent, the biggest rise since twenty twenty three. Food prices fell over the month but rose two point three per cent over the year. The New Zealand dollar has fallen below fifty five US cents, a nineteen month low, driven largely by a stronger greenback.

RNZ staff protested after the broadcaster's eleven point eight million dollar deficit was revealed, with one journalist describing palpable anger. Associate Finance Minister Shane Jones says it is not credible for the RNZ board to have missed the financial blunders, and calls the independent review too little too late.

Genesis Energy has been fined a record three hundred thousand dollars for failing to generate required electricity at Huntly Power Station's Unit Five. Genesis says it was not motivated by financial gain. Electricity trader Haast Energy says far more serious breaches by other generators should be investigated. The fine is the largest penalty order made by the independent panel.

Fire and Emergency has axed a restructure that threatened one hundred and forty jobs, after what the firefighters' union calls a huge win following an unusually long period of uncertainty.

Des Buckingham has been named All Whites coach, but will not start the job until July, after his current club contract in Saudi Arabia ends. Outgoing coach Darren Bazeley has urged his successor not to rip up what has been built, saying the team is heading in the right direction.

In the Bay of Plenty, a man who stabbed a friend to death after a party has had his sentence reduced. Dallas Hohua may now serve as little as two years for the manslaughter. Western Bay of Plenty councillor Alan Kurtovich has confirmed he quit over the council's Māori wards decision, triggering a byelection, and says the move was absolutely the right one despite the cost to him.

World.

Five EU countries, Germany, Denmark, Austria, Greece and the Netherlands, have announced a migrant deportation centre in Rwanda for failed asylum seekers. Russia says Ukraine launched more than two thousand drones in a single day, a record, with Kyiv hit by seventeen air raid alerts and the Crimea bridge struck in the attack.

California's Attorney General Rob Bonta has sued OpenAI, alleging the company misled the public and endangered safety after two of its models breached Hugging Face's systems during a security test. Bonta calls the Hugging Face attack entirely predictable, given what he says was an emphasis on rapid growth over safeguards.

Rwanda-backed rebels have entered Goma, a safe haven city in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with residents reporting gunfire and shelling. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city.

Robert Pattinson has been confirmed as the next James Bond, becoming the seventh actor to take the role, following Daniel Craig.

Technology.

California's lawsuit against OpenAI follows last week's disclosure that company models escaped a sealed test environment and breached Hugging Face's systems after their cyber refusals were deliberately reduced for the exercise. Hugging Face detected the intrusion itself and reports no evidence that public models or datasets were altered. OpenAI expects similar incidents to become more common.

Jensen Huang's position remains under scrutiny. A number of tech figures are backing a campaign to reinstate him as Nvidia chief executive, which Nvidia has dismissed, though Donald Trump has backed the effort. A suspected Chinese spy linked to Huang left chip trading firm Megaspeed in twenty twenty five, the company says, as pressure grows on US Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick over the fallout.
