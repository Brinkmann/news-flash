New Zealand.

The Waitangi Tribunal has found the Crown had no clear plan for Māori health after scrapping Te Aka Whai Ora, the Māori Health Authority. The Tribunal says the Crown told claimants, itself and the public for nearly eighteen months that alternative plans were coming, without one existing. This adds pressure to a government already facing scrutiny over transparency at the Ministry of Education.

The Ombudsman will investigate allegations that Ministry of Education officials tried to circumvent the Official Information Act, after leaked footage showed a manager telling staff not to write things down. Secretary for Education Ellen MacGregor-Reid says she cannot explain the comments and is "not happy" about them. Education Minister Erica Stanford has separately refused to release her curriculum feedback, saying the information does not exist. Principals and te reo Māori advocates say the secrecy is unwarranted.

Mayors around the country say they were caught off guard by the government's about turn on council amalgamation. Wellington's mayor points to a lack of support inside government. The local government minister disputes that reading.

New Zealand First has confirmed Māori Development Minister Shane Jones backs the party's selection of Murray Chong as its New Plymouth candidate. Chong, a veteran councillor, abstained from a Māori ward vote in 2024, citing threats to his life. The Reserve Bank has asked for a report into TSB's capital and liquidity calculations, ahead of Heartland Group shareholders voting next week on a proposal to buy TSB from the Toi Foundation.

Fonterra has reported a three point four billion dollar operating profit for the year, its first full year since selling the Mainland brands business. Outgoing chief executive Miles Hurrell was paid around eight point two million dollars over the same period.

In the Bay of Plenty, Western Bay of Plenty councillor Alan Kurtovich says a Māori wards decision was the reason he quit, triggering a byelection. Dallas Hohua, convicted of manslaughter for stabbing a friend to death after a party, has had his sentence reduced and may serve as little as two years.

Technology.

An international bounty programme has put forty thousand dollars on a group of hackers responsible for last year's attack on New Zealand's health system. The reward aims to draw information from within criminal networks, a tactic increasingly used against ransomware gangs. Separately, the FBI is investigating a claim that hackers stole personal details, including blood and urine test results, for all its agents, which experts say could expose them to blackmail.
