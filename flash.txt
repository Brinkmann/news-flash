New Zealand.

The election remains unresolved two days on. Economists say household finances now hinge on how coalition talks play out, with the first full Budget in May the real test of which promises survive. Business groups say the uncertainty is frustrating firms that delayed decisions hoping for a clear result, though analysts say financial markets are more worried about global factors than New Zealand's outcome. National's Tom Rutherford won the new Mount Maunganui electorate, beating Labour's Rhieve Grey by fourteen thousand and fifty nine votes. Todd McClay became Rotorua's longest serving MP, beating Fisher Wang by five thousand three hundred and one votes, ahead of special votes still to be counted. Luxon says he will call Opportunity's Qiulae Wong shortly, and defends his leadership after National lost four senior MPs, including Chris Bishop.

Severe thunderstorm warnings are in force for the upper North Island, with MetService warning of downpours up to forty millimetres an hour. A flash flood warning is also current for the Far North and Auckland. One person died in a crash in South Waikato, with four others taken to Rotorua Hospital, one in a serious condition.

In Western Bay of Plenty, Tauranga councillor Alan Kurtovich has confirmed his resignation over the council's Māori wards decision triggered the byelection, saying the call was right despite the cost.

World.

Israel's president has given opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot first chance to form the next government, passing over Benjamin Netanyahu. Eisenkot has warned Israel is at a point of no return for its democracy, and has not ruled out Netanyahu joining his government.

Seven people died and more than one hundred were injured when two trains collided outside Giza in Egypt, with several carriages derailed. President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi has ordered an investigation and says anyone found negligent will be held accountable.

Houthi forces say at least thirty three people were killed in a Saudi air strike. Saudi Arabia has not commented. It follows a week of escalating strikes in the region, with Iran warning it will, in its words, rain down hell if the United States escalates further.

King Charles led Remembrance Sunday services at the Cenotaph in London, with ten thousand armed forces veterans attending, including twelve from the Second World War.

Technology.

Global share markets fell sharply last week on fears of an artificial intelligence spending slowdown, before paring losses as AI stocks climbed back after trillions were wiped off valuations. Analysts say United States midterm results, which weakened Republican control, have fuelled expectations of tougher scrutiny of data centre construction and renewed debate over how far AI investment can keep expanding unchecked.

Binance says the six point three billion US dollar hack of its exchange used fake job interviews, contacting an engineer through a legitimate job hunting platform before inviting him to bogus interviews with fake company staff.
