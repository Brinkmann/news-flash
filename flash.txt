New Zealand.

Police divers recovered a body from the water following a crash on Chapel Street, Tauranga overnight, Bay of Plenty Police confirm. The vehicle entered the water in the incident. Western Bay of Plenty faces a byelection after Alan Kurtovich quit the district council. He says his decision followed the council's Māori wards vote, and was, in his words, absolutely the right one despite the cost.

Fuel prices have topped four dollars a litre at the pump. An AA spokesperson says prices are expected to stay high until at least Christmas. Police have confirmed one person died after becoming trapped in an avalanche on Mount Dixon in Canterbury on Sunday morning.

On the campaign trail, National has pledged to remove the right to silence when a child under twelve is murdered, making it a Crimes Act offence not to disclose information to police about who was present. Defence lawyers say it flips the burden of proof and flies in the face of justice. Labour has pledged seven million dollars to get children off screens and into sport and recreation programmes. NZ First is backing a commuter rail trial in Canterbury, leader Winston Peters saying the existing railway lines make it achievable. The latest Poll of Polls shows National still leads but the left bloc has gained ground, with three polls now putting the Opportunities Party in a kingmaker position. The election is November the seventh.

A new survey finds Māori are going without healthcare or delaying treatment because of cost and availability. Documents show police investigators uploaded three hundred and fifty thousand lines of Chinese audio to an unsecured Google Translate workaround, a risky practice flagged in internal reports. Fire alarm delays hit Auckland's City Rail Link at Karanga-a-Hape station, with East West trains initially skipping three central stations.

World.

Protests in France turned to riots and looting over the weekend, with burnt out cars and shattered glass left on Paris streets. About one thousand eight hundred people were arrested nationwide. The unrest stems from anger over school conditions and living costs. Russian strikes on St Petersburg hit oil facilities and a data centre overnight, killing at least one person, as Donald Trump waives several sanctions on Russia amid his row with President Zelensky.

Rwanda backed M23 rebels have entered Goma in the Democratic Republic of Congo. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city, with residents reporting gunfire as Congolese troops were overrun. Israeli settlers attacked European Union diplomats visiting olive harvesters in the occupied West Bank. The European Union has condemned the attack and demanded accountability, while Israel's military called the violence condemnable.

At least seventeen people have died in a crush at a Hindu festival event in Kolkata, India, with many more injured. Donald Trump has endorsed Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ahead of the October twenty seventh election, calling him a strong, brave and brilliant leader.

Technology.

Anthropic is seeking new investors ahead of a planned stock market listing, as some investors grow concerned about the scale of spending on artificial intelligence infrastructure. No timeline has been given. Fashion retail group TFG London, owner of Phase Eight and Hobbs, has confirmed a cybersecurity incident disrupting customers, while H and M is separately investigating a hack claim. A Russian man arrested in Germany is accused of involvement in the twenty twenty four Synnovis hack, which disrupted blood testing and patient care across London hospitals.

Sport.

League. Rugby League World Cup. The Kiwi Ferns beat Fiji in their opening match, doing most of the attacking in the second half before finally breaking Fijian resistance late. Tonga beat England thirty to twelve, backing up their historic first win over England from the tournament opener. Fiji's Bati also played the Cook Islands' Aitu in Saturday's action.

Rugby. All Blacks loose forward Peter Lakai was prominent in Sydney during the Bledisloe sweep and is pressing his claim for a long run in the number seven jersey ahead of next month's Nations Championship tests. Canterbury beat Taranaki thirty eight to twenty eight in Christchurch to book a spot in the National Provincial Championship final, joining Northland.

Netball. The Silver Ferns beat the Australian Diamonds fifty six to fifty in Auckland in the opening test of the Constellation Cup, extending the Diamonds' losing run on New Zealand soil.

Cricket. The White Ferns were beaten by Australia in the opening Twenty20 international in Sydney, despite a breakthrough innings from opener Georgia Plimmer.

Golf. New Zealander Daniel Hillier shot a final round sixty seven to finish runner up at the DP World India Championship, moving closer to a two thousand and twenty seven PGA Tour card. Spain's Eugenio Chacarra won the event by two strokes to move top of the Race to Dubai standings ahead of Patrick Reed and Rory McIlroy.

Football. Manchester City went six points clear at the top of the Premier League, Erling Haaland scoring twice in a three two win over Ipswich at the Etihad. Arsenal beat Nottingham Forest one nil, Noni Madueke scoring the only goal late on.
