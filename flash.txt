A High Court ruling that the government breached women's rights over pay equity changes has forced ministers to reckon with Parliament on two fronts, and Anthropic's new shares have dived eleven percent as a wider tech sell off hits Wall Street.

New Zealand.

The High Court has ruled the coalition's changes to the Equal Pay Act discriminated against women and breached the Bill of Rights Act. The changes scrapped dozens of pay equity claims and made new ones harder to lodge. Workers' groups have welcomed the ruling. A former National MP says Parliament must now address pay equity on two separate fronts, through legislation and through the claims themselves.

NZ First list MP Michael Laws is refusing calls to quit Parliament after mocking an Opportunity Party opponent's appearance and making a sexual remark about her. He says he will resist what he calls tyranny. Winston Peters has not forced him out. Labour has separately distanced itself from colleague Willie Jackson's comments that the United States ambassador should apologise or be sent packing over his remarks about Auckland mayor Wayne Brown.

A new report says the government could forgo up to twelve billion dollars in revenue by 2035 if it fails to fix the Emissions Trading Scheme. Dunedin councillor Benedict Ong has cost his council sixty seven thousand dollars defending four code of conduct complaints he calls political and retaliatory. Oranga Tamariki documents show staff feared being seen as snitches over colleagues' sexual behaviour with teenagers at a facility.

Fonterra has raised its forecast farmgate milk price, narrowing the range to between nine dollars sixty and ten dollars sixty per kilogram of milk solids, citing strong Chinese demand. Turners Automotive posted a record first half profit despite what it calls the worst oil price shock since the nineteen seventies.

In the Bay of Plenty, former Filthy Few gang president James Henry Wilson has been denied parole. He is serving a life sentence for the 1999 murder of Jo-Anne van Duyvenbooden in Tauranga. He told the Parole Board he has changed, moving from gang life to gardening.

World.

A Russian missile hit Poland during a Ukraine allied military exercise. Nobody was hurt and no damage was reported, but Warsaw's Donald Tusk called it a provocation from Moscow. It follows one of Russia's biggest air attacks yet on Kyiv, days after Ukraine struck the Kerch Bridge.

The United Kingdom and European Union have agreed a deal covering British food exports, emissions trading and a youth experience scheme. UK leader Burnham says he will explore even closer ties, though BBC analysis suggests shoppers should not expect lower prices soon.

Rwanda backed rebels have entered Goma in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with residents reporting gunfire and shelling. The United Nations estimates more than one million displaced people are now inside the city, after Congolese troops were overrun.

Technology.

Anthropic shares fell eleven percent and Nvidia dropped twelve percent, dragging down the Nasdaq nearly five percent, as a wider technology sell off continued following Anthropic's stock market debut this week. BBC analysis questions whether the float can ease fears of a broader AI investment bubble.

United States President Trump has threatened a tax on American technology firms that build AI data centres overseas, saying firms doing so could face what he called a tremendous tax. He gave little policy detail. Separately, California has opened a probe into Nvidia over AI chip exports to China, following the departure of former chief executive Jensen Huang.

Sport.

Cricket. Black Caps versus India, first test at the Basin Reserve, into day three. Henry Nicholls and Glenn Phillips both scored centuries to resurrect the innings after early batting collapse.

Rugby. Beauden Barrett plays his hundred and fiftieth test as the All Blacks face England at Twickenham, three ten a.m. New Zealand time Sunday. Coach Dave Rennie has praised Barrett's form after he was overlooked earlier in the season. England are boosted by returning players.

Mixed martial arts. Navajo Stirling fights Jiří Procházka in Qatar this weekend. A win would put him in line for a title shot against his own teammate, Carlos Ulberg.

Basketball. New Zealand's men have taken an unassailable two nil series lead over the Silver Ferns, winning sixty four to forty six in Hamilton.

Football. Wellington Phoenix beat Western Sydney Wanderers two nil for a third straight A-League win. Auckland FC coach Pedro Moreira says watching the All Whites train gave him fresh ideas for his own side's campaign.
