New Zealand.

The Tauranga landslides inquiry has been delayed because agencies were too slow supplying information, Tauranga City Council confirms. A separate review it commissioned came in about two months behind schedule. The extension puts pressure on the inquiry's final deadline.

Labour has pledged faster emergency department wait times and an immediate review of unused hospital beds, leader Chris Hipkins saying the party will make hospitals accountable for getting patients care. Christopher Luxon and Hipkins meet for their final election debate on health, with Hipkins also challenging Luxon directly over the state of the health system. Neither leader will commit to having a government formed by Christmas, raising the prospect of coalition talks running past the holiday period.

New Zealand's free trade agreement with India has officially begun. Trade Minister Todd McClay says fifty seven percent of exports to India are now tariff free, including sheep meat, wool, coal and most forestry products. Businesses are being urged to look beyond traditional exports, with digital services among the sectors expected to benefit.

A body has been recovered from the water following a crash on Chapel Street, Tauranga. Bay of Plenty Police confirmed the recovery after the vehicle entered the water overnight. Separately, Dallas Hohua has had his manslaughter sentence reduced and may now serve as little as two years, for stabbing a friend to death after a party.

Kiwibank workers are on strike over a pay offer. Their union says the bank's two point two five percent offer, in their words, doesn't add up, and that Kiwibank can afford more. BNZ's senior economist says economic recovery remains slow, with employment and retail spending still contracting under pressure on household incomes.

A new survey from BNZ puts annual ownership costs for a new house purchase at about thirty five percent of median household income, down from a peak of fifty percent in twenty twenty three.

World.

Rwanda backed M23 rebels have entered Goma in the Democratic Republic of Congo. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city, with residents reporting gunfire as Congolese troops were overrun.

An Australian independent MP, Helen Haines, remains in hospital after being hit by a car in what the prime minister says may have been a deliberate attack.
