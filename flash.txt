A magnitude five point nine earthquake near Taumarunui and a Commerce Commission case against Air New Zealand lead this morning, while Trump, Xi and Putin meet in China.

New Zealand.

The Commerce Commission has filed charges against Air New Zealand, alleging the airline misled passengers about compensation rights when flights were disrupted for reasons within its control, going back to January 2024. Air New Zealand has not yet responded publicly. The case now goes before the courts.

An independent review has found Selwyn District Council's water billing system under-billed by close to three million dollars across thirteen hundred properties, with widespread weaknesses in historic record keeping. About one hundred and fifteen properties remain unresolved as of Monday. The council says it is working through the backlog.

A tsunami advisory was issued for parts of New Zealand at five forty Wednesday evening after unusual wave activity, then cancelled fifteen minutes later. Earth Sciences is investigating the cause. No damage reported.

Christopher Luxon is in Shenzhen for a twenty three hour APEC visit, as Trump, Xi and Putin hold their first joint meeting on Thursday. Winston Peters has separately said he is deeply concerned after an investigation, backed by the international chemical weapons watchdog, found Russia used tear gas against Ukrainian troops.

Wayne Brown has drawn criticism after telling Chinese state television that democracy had, in his words, had its day, and appeared to briefly refer to himself as a prime minister. A law professor has separately criticised the United States ambassador for attacking Brown over the comments. Brown has called for a rethink of MMP following the trip.

In the Bay of Plenty, Western Bay of Plenty councillor Alan Kurtovich has confirmed he quit over the council's Māori wards decision, triggering a byelection. A man who stabbed a friend to death after a party has had his sentence reduced, and may serve as little as two years for the manslaughter.

World.

A magnitude five point nine earthquake struck near Taumarunui, the largest recent tremor felt across the country, shallow, with aftershocks continuing. No major damage reported.

Israel's military has launched a ground operation in Gaza beyond the agreed Yellow Line, which it says is dismantling a tunnel network. Hamas has called the incursion a flagrant ceasefire violation. Palestinians had been streaming back into northern Gaza on foot after Israel opened a crossing earlier this week.

Rwanda backed rebels have entered Goma in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with residents reporting gunfire and shelling after Congolese troops were overrun. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city.

UK inflation has hit its highest rate in a year, with a typical household energy bill forecast to rise four hundred and forty three pounds annually from January, a twenty six percent jump. The government has separately backed a welfare shake up aimed at preventing what it calls a lost generation of young people out of work.

Technology.

Anthropic is preparing a record breaking stock market listing expected to place it among the ten most valuable companies in the United States, with its founders becoming multi billionaires. The float comes as analysts question whether it can ease fears of an artificial intelligence investment bubble. Nvidia has confirmed it will not reinstate Jensen Huang as chief executive despite a campaign backed by Silicon Valley figures and President Trump, reporting strong quarterly sales regardless.

Grand Theft Auto six launched today, with New Zealand first in the world to get access. Sensor Tower estimates the game has already made one point one eight billion US dollars from pre-orders. Local retailers expect a flow on boost to trading through to Christmas and say console supply may be squeezed.

World Rugby's crackdown on hip and knee drop tackles continues to draw reaction after Springboks coach Rassie Erasmus was banned six matches.

Formula One. Valtteri Bottas has been confirmed at Cadillac for the twenty twenty seven season. Liam Lawson has revealed he was dropped from the Red Bull junior programme for three days in twenty nineteen after a hot headed moment.

Tennis. Carlos Alcaraz beat world number five Daniil Medvedev at the ATP Finals in London despite an injury concern, continuing his late season form.

Football. The Football Ferns have named three uncapped players in their squad for a two match series against the Philippines, ahead of next year's World Cup.

Basketball. New Zealand Breakers signing Skylar Mays has been cleared to make his debut today against his former club.
