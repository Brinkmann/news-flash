New Zealand.

World.

Saudi Arabia says its coalition struck a Houthi fuel depot near Sanaa, with missiles intercepted near Riyadh in response. It follows last week's Houthi strikes on Riyadh's airport that killed thirteen people. Donald Trump says the Houthis are deliberately trying to drive up oil prices before next month's United States midterm elections.

Pope Leo has had surgery to remove a lower lobe of his right lung after a lung cancer diagnosis. The Vatican says the surgery was successful.

Rwanda backed rebels have entered Goma, Congo's safe haven city, with residents reporting gunfire and shelling. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city, adding pressure on aid efforts already stretched by the conflict.

A merchant ship has sunk in the Black Sea after a drone attack. Bulgaria has summoned Russia's ambassador. Six crew were rescued, ten remain missing.

Technology.

Fridges in French supermarkets were hit by a cyber attack overnight, spoiling fresh and frozen food estimated to be worth millions of euros across hundreds of stores. A New Zealand cyber security expert says the incident is a wake up call for supermarkets here, warning similar refrigeration and stock systems are vulnerable to the same kind of intrusion.

Microsoft president Brad Smith says artificial intelligence is less regulated than milk, calling for global rules, with Britain in the best position to lead on them. It follows Britain's AI minister warning a cyber breach of the country's defences is likely. Scientists have separately urged tighter international AI controls, citing polling that shows doubt in political leaders' ability to manage the risks.
