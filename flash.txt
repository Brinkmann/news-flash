A magnitude five point nine avalanche fatality on Mount Dixon and a widening election poll lead for National headline today's news.

New Zealand.

A person has died after being trapped in an avalanche on Mount Dixon in Canterbury on Sunday morning, police confirm. The latest 1News-Verian poll shows National's lead over Labour widening, with Labour slipping one point to twenty seven percent. Election day is November the seventh.

Christopher Luxon and Chris Hipkins clashed in their final leaders' debate on Tuesday night, trading barbs over health and homelessness. Luxon called Hipkins "the most abysmal education minister", Hipkins said Luxon was out of touch. Newsroom's fact check found misleading claims from both leaders. Neither has committed to forming a government by Christmas.

A new report shows four in ten major Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment projects missed their targets in the last year, including a fifty million dollar Biometric Capability Update. The ministry blames "planning optimism". Two immigration projects were among the failures.

New Zealand has signed a one point six billion dollar deal to buy five navy Seahawk helicopters, fitted with anti-submarine torpedoes and air-surface missiles. Former navy officer Darren Katipa, who held high-level national security clearance, has been jailed over a one and a half tonne cocaine plot linked to the Comancheros off the Tauranga coast.

A body has been recovered from the water following a crash on Chapel Street in Tauranga overnight, Bay of Plenty Police confirm. Western Bay of Plenty councillor Alan Kurtovich has resigned over the district's Māori wards decision, triggering a byelection.

World.

Rwanda-backed M23 rebels have entered Goma in the Democratic Republic of Congo. United Nations officials estimate more than one million displaced people are now inside the city, with residents reporting gunfire as Congolese troops were overrun.

Sudanese army drones have hit a funeral convoy and a fuel market, killing more than three hundred people, sources say. The truck was reportedly carrying mourners, and the market was alleged to be supplying fuel to the Rapid Support Forces.

A seventh day of clashes between protesters and police has hit Albania, with almost three hundred people injured, more than half of them police. Marseille is bracing for more unrest after a weekend of what officials called apocalyptic disorder spread across French cities.

An Australian man has been charged over the attack on independent MP Helen Haines, who remains critical after being struck by a car outside her electorate office. His family say they have known her for more than twenty years.

Technology.

The United States has banned Chinese AI model DeepSeek from government agencies, deepening the technology race with Beijing. China's own growth has accelerated, with exports lifted by its AI boom offsetting weak domestic spending.

A Google cloud outage disrupted UK banks and websites, with Revolut among the worst affected as banking services went down for many customers. Musk and Anthropic have both pledged public share giveaways, Anthropic's ahead of its planned stock market listing, after investor concern over the scale of AI infrastructure spending.

Sport.

Rugby League World Cup. The Kiwis and Kiwi Ferns have named five debutants for Sunday's double header against France. Christchurch front-rower Tanner Stowers-Smith will make his Kiwis debut, seventy five years after his great-grandfather last wore the jersey. Tenika Willison and Chantay Kiria-Ratu are among the Kiwi Ferns debutants.

Rugby. Black Ferns coach Whitney Hansen expects a French backlash in the second test after a record rout in the first, and is set to make several changes. Leroy Carter remains out of the All Blacks' Northern Tour as the injury list grows. Siale Lauaki has re-signed with the Hurricanes. Tevita Mafileo, once on the All Blacks radar, has rejoined the Chiefs after leaving the Hurricanes.

Netball. The Silver Ferns say they respect, but do not fear, the Australian Diamonds ahead of their next meeting, pointing to recent form despite several players competing in the Australian league.

Cricket. The White Ferns lost to Australia, unable to press their attack while chasing one hundred and sixty eight for victory.

Formula One. Liam Lawson will headline VCARB garage sessions as part of his build-up to the United States Grand Prix in Texas this weekend. Former Red Bull boss Christian Horner has reportedly written in a new memoir that he was uncomfortable with Lawson's promotion to the top team at the start of this season.

Motorsport. New Zealand MotoGP rookie Cormac Buchanan is managing a forced lay-off as a difficult first season winds down.

Coming up. The Rugby League World Cup double header runs Sunday, with the Kiwis and Kiwi Ferns both in action. The Black Ferns play France in the second test this week. The United States Grand Prix runs this weekend. Labour Day falls on Monday.
