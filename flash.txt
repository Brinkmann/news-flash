New Zealand's Defence Minister has lost her Chief of Defence and her Fire and Emergency boss in the space of a week, as the election campaign is rocked by a second Labour candidate resignation.

New Zealand.

Labour candidate Kerrin Leoni has broken her silence after resigning over a photo showing her with family members and gang associates. She says her whānau has been turned into a political weapon. Newsroom reports more Labour resignations could follow before polling day, and commentators say the left bloc has lost control of the campaign in its final fortnight. Chris Hipkins has also accused National of double standards over a separate photo involving minister Paul Goldsmith's colleague Potaka.

The Green Party has released its fiscal plan, promising to lift health spending by three billion dollars a year. Co-leader Chloe Swarbrick unveiled the costings at Parliament. National has countered with its own Plan for Parents policy, including financial literacy in the school curriculum, launched by Christopher Luxon in Auckland.

Air Marshal Tony Davies has resigned as Chief of Defence over what the Defence Minister called an inappropriate relationship, with new reporting from Newsroom detailing a letter he received two days before quitting. Fire and Emergency chief executive Kerry Gregory has also resigned effective immediately, after losing his minister's confidence in September over withheld information. Both departures were reported by RNZ and Newsroom.

Oil prices have hit their highest level since May on reports the United States is again weighing strikes on Iran. That matters locally because petrol has been driving inflation, and the Reserve Bank's Official Cash Rate decision, expected to lift rates by twenty five basis points, lands this week.

In Tauranga, Rotorua Lakes Council has approved more than eight hundred thousand dollars in repairs to the Prince's Gate Archway, calling it an immediate risk that needed to be removed. Western Bay of Plenty's byelection continues, triggered by Alan Kurtovich's resignation over the district's Māori wards decision, which he says was still the right call.

World.

A magnitude seven point two earthquake struck off Vanuatu. A tsunami warning was issued and later cancelled, a matter of record from RNZ.

Rwanda backed M23 rebels have entered the Congolese city of Goma, with residents reporting ongoing gunfire and shelling. The United Nations estimates more than one million displaced people are now inside the city, according to the Wall Street Journal.

Israel's prime minister has ordered a major foreign policy shift amid a diplomatic row with the United Kingdom, instructing diplomats to raise the Falkland Islands' status directly with Argentina. Reported by Stuff and Al Jazeera's wider Middle East coverage of rising regional tension.

Israeli forces have begun a full lockdown of the occupied West Bank after the killing of two Israeli soldiers on Friday. More than two thousand people have been displaced from Hebron as homes are demolished, officials say. Reported by BBC and Al Jazeera.

Technology.

A critical flaw in the widely used PHP programming language has triggered a global cyber security scramble, now extending beyond its initial days, with researchers saying artificial intelligence helped uncover vulnerabilities described as among the most significant ever found. The UK government says five hundred new vulnerabilities have turned up in its own systems, and IT workers describe the response as relentless. Reported by BBC Technology, continuing from earlier in the week.

Elon Musk is considering merging Tesla and SpaceX, with special committees at both companies examining the idea. BBC reports it would be the biggest corporate merger in history if it proceeds. No timeline has been given.

Sport.

Rugby League World Cup. Tonga beat Lebanon thirty six to nil in Sydney, their second win of the tournament, a matter of record. Fiji's Bulikula beat the Papua New Guinea Orchids in Port Moresby. Police are asking fans to keep victory celebrations within reasonable hours after complaints about parades running into the early hours.

League. The Kiwis play Cook Islands on Sunday, described as a chance for young players to press their claims, with bigger selection questions in play. The Australian Rugby League has confirmed it will keep the PNG Chiefs name for its NRL franchise despite a legal challenge from New Zealand Rugby.

Rugby. The Black Ferns beat France thirty one to twelve in Whangarei, scoring three tries in the last fifteen minutes against a much-changed French side, a matter of record. All Blacks forwards Shannon Frizell, Tyrel Lomax and Samisoni Taukei'aho have been ruled out of the upcoming United Kingdom tour with leg and neck injuries. Northland face Canterbury in Monday's NPC final in Christchurch, having already beaten them once this season.

Cricket. India beat the Black Caps by four wickets in the second T twenty, taking a two nil series lead, a matter of record. Glenn Phillips top scored but could not close out the chase.

Football. Auckland FC conceded five goals in a loss to Western Sydney, the first defeat under new coach Pedro Moreira.

Tennis. Alexandra Eala, inside the world's top twenty, is confirmed for January's ASB Classic in Auckland, with another top twenty player also entering.

Coming up. The Kiwis play Cook Islands on Sunday. The Northland versus Canterbury NPC final is Monday in Christchurch. The Reserve Bank's Official Cash Rate decision lands this week. Election day is November the seventh.
