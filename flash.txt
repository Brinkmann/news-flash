Costco's South Island store has moved a step closer, and the two Chrises traded blows in last night's leaders debate while their finance spokespeople clashed in Queenstown.

New Zealand.

The government has given Costco the go ahead to lodge a fast track application for a megastore near Christchurch Airport, its first South Island site. The minister confirmed the approval yesterday. The application still needs to go through the fast track process before construction can begin.

Christopher Luxon and Chris Hipkins faced off in the Herald leaders debate last night, both men claiming under questioning that they had never lied in politics. A Newsroom fact check found false claims from both leaders. Separately, Labour and National finance spokespeople Barbara Edmonds and Nicola Willis clashed over the government's Investment Boost scheme in a Queenstown debate, with Edmonds unveiling a Labour plan for up to fifty million dollars a year for a venture capital fund.

Starship Hospital has suspended some child cancer treatments because of a nursing shortage. Health New Zealand confirmed treatments are being rescheduled, though it will not say whether chemotherapy or bone marrow transplants are affected. A hospital nurse said the delay increases the risk of complications or death. Reported by RNZ and the Herald.

Watercare has apologised after mistakenly taking six million dollars early from almost forty four thousand Auckland customer accounts during a billing system upgrade. The utility says affected customers will be reimbursed.

Immigration New Zealand has sharply increased refusals of overseas student visa applications. In the past three months seventy per cent of applications from Bangladesh were declined, and thirty nine per cent from India, nearly double the normal rate. The agency cites fraud concerns. Reported by RNZ and the Herald.

In the Bay of Plenty, police have charged a twenty five year old man with murder following Tuesday's fatal crash on the Te Puke Highway. The homicide investigation continues. Western Bay of Plenty's Māori wards byelection has been triggered after councillor Alan Kurtovich quit, saying the wards decision was the reason, a council record confirmed through the Bay of Plenty Times.

World.

Rwanda backed M23 rebels have entered Goma, Congo's safe haven city, after overrunning Congolese troops. The United Nations estimates more than one million displaced people are now inside the city. Former president Joseph Kabila has joined forces with the rebels, widening the conflict. Reported by the BBC, Al Jazeera and the Wall Street Journal.

Pope Leo is recovering well after surgery to remove part of his right lung following a lung cancer diagnosis. Doctors say he is walking and recovering as expected after Tuesday's ninety minute procedure.

A container ship has sunk in the Strait of Hormuz after being hit by a projectile. All twenty one crew of the Singapore flagged vessel were evacuated safely, Singapore maritime authorities say.

Hundreds of thousands of public sector workers are on strike across the Republic of Ireland in an ongoing pay dispute with the Irish government.

Technology.

A Saudi employee has been charged with planting a hidden recording device, wrapped in toilet paper, in a USB port in an OpenAI executive conference room. Prosecutors say the man, Abdulrahman Alfozan, had worked at the company for more than a year before his arrest by the F.B.I. The case raises questions about corporate espionage inside leading AI firms.

Netflix will cut about one thousand jobs, around six per cent of its global workforce, its steepest cuts since twenty twenty two. The company has not detailed which teams are affected.

An Anthropic AI agent gave Philadelphia police a fake tip in an unsolved murder case. Police said the tip was flagged as spam, but criticised Anthropic for taking more than two months to detect and report the breach. Microsoft president Brad Smith says artificial intelligence remains less regulated than milk and has called for global rules.

Sport.

Rugby League World Cup. All eight Pacific Island teams have named their line ups for the opening round this weekend. The Kiwis face defending champions Australia in Sydney on Thursday, with Jahrome Hughes back in the squad. All Blacks captain Ardie Savea has shared advice with Kiwis co-captains Joseph Tapine and James Fisher Harris ahead of the tournament.

Rugby. The All Blacks continue preparing for Saturday's second Bledisloe Cup test against the Wallabies in Sydney. Jordie Barrett will be considered an All Blacks great, according to assistant coach Tana Umaga. Jake White has been named head coach of the Flying Fijians, replacing Senirusi Seruvakula.

Basketball. The Breakers beat the defending champion Sydney Kings one hundred and two to one hundred and one in Canberra. Carlin Davison posted career best numbers for the Tall Blacks forward. The Breakers are still waiting on a new import signing, with Sam Mennenga filling the gap.

Cricket. West Indies beat India by twenty four runs to level their Twenty20 series, with Shai Hope scoring an unbeaten one hundred and two including nine sixes, a record century. The White Ferns begin a new era against Australia, following the retirement of three senior players and a coaching change.

Coming up. The Kiwis play the Kangaroos in Sydney on Thursday in their Rugby League World Cup opener. The All Blacks face the Wallabies in Sydney on Saturday. The election campaign continues with the final leaders debate next Tuesday in Auckland.
