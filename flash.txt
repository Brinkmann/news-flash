Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang has returned to the company less than a week after resigning amid scandal, adding close to one trillion US dollars to its share price and dragging global markets up with it.

New Zealand.

The ministry of immigration wants a new biometrics system after a fifty million dollar failure, capable of ten thousand photo comparisons an hour against twenty million templates. The old system could not cope. A procurement process is expected to follow.

David Seymour wants a tougher, faster inquiry into RNZ's financial failures completed by Christmas. Labour wants the whole board sacked and replaced instead. ACT is separately pushing a referendum on abolishing the Māori seats during coalition talks, a proposal a political scientist warns could trigger considerable protest action.

Simeon Brown's office has denied he ordered health workforce data held back until after the election. The figures show just one hundred and seventeen more full time nurses were hired for the year.

Teacher aides rallied in Wellington, their union calling pay equity law changes dystopian for low paid women, saying rates have dropped in real terms since a twenty twenty settlement. Christopher Luxon says the next government will decide the issue's future.

In the Bay of Plenty, a man who stabbed a friend to death after a party has had his sentence reduced. Dallas Hohua may now serve as little as two years for the manslaughter. And Western Bay of Plenty councillor Alan Kurtovich has quit over the district's Māori wards decision, triggering a byelection he says was worth the cost.

World.

The COP thirty one climate talks have collapsed in Turkiye after running fifty hours over time, with the European Union, United Kingdom and Pacific nations saying no deal was better than one ignoring fossil fuels. Green co-leader Chlöe Swarbrick backed the collapse. New Zealand's climate minister says the country stood ready to support the deal that fell over.

Rwanda backed rebels have entered Goma in the Democratic Republic of Congo, overrunning Congolese troops amid gunfire and shelling. The United Nations estimates more than one million displaced people are now inside the city. Fighting continues, with no ceasefire in sight.

Ukraine has released video of strikes on Russian vessels off Libya, with President Zelensky saying a Russian warship was sunk and a cargo ship hit. It follows a suspected drone attack on a Russian ship at Benghazi reported earlier this week, raising the stakes of the war in a new theatre.

Christopher Luxon has called Vladimir Putin a killer, after Donald Trump met the Russian president at the APEC summit in China and described him as a friend. Separately, the United States has added Singapore based Megaspeed to its export control list, accused of funnelling AI chips to China after a former executive's affair with Huang became public.

Technology.

Jensen Huang is back as Nvidia chief executive, days after resigning over the fallout of an affair involving an alleged spy. Donald Trump says he brokered the deal. The reinstatement added nearly one trillion US dollars to Nvidia's value and lifted the Nasdaq more than six per cent. New York mayor Zohran Mamdani criticised the volatility of AI linked stocks. New Zealand's own share market rebounded on the back of the Wall Street rally.

Allied Press, publisher of the Otago Daily Times, was hit by a cyber attack in the early hours, leaving staff facing blank screens. Teams are working to restore access in time to produce tomorrow's paper.

Sport.

Rugby. All Black loose forward Luke Jacobson will leave New Zealand rugby next year, departing for France. Jordie Barrett is a finalist for World Player of the Year, with Peter Lakai's try against the Wallabies up for Try of the Year. Highlanders coach Jamie Joseph has signed through to twenty thirty one, saying ambitions of the All Blacks job played no part. The players' union says the All Blacks' record seventeen game schedule was always hugely ambitious, after Sir Graham Henry said players were exhausted following back to back defeats.

Cricket. The Black Caps held on with their last five wickets to deny India victory in the first test, India having led by three hundred and fifty two runs after the first innings. New Zealand now turn to the second test.

Hockey. India have won the centenary series against the Black Sticks, taking it with a win and two draws as the teams marked one hundred years of sporting ties.

Sailing. The Black Foils beat a difficult SailGP season to win in Dubai, with emotional scenes on board after the result.

Netball. Former Silver Ferns captain Julie Carter has died, aged sixty, remembered as competitive, clever and committed. New Zealand netball has now lost two former Silver Fern captains this year.

Football. Brentford beat ten man Everton three nil, with Everton captain James Tarkowski sent off for the first time in his Premier League career. Manchester United continue to search for a response under Mauricio Pochettino after last week's heavy defeat.

Coming up. The Black Caps prepare for the second test against India. Highlanders fans will see Jamie Joseph's long term deal take shape next season. Rugby followers await further comment on the All Blacks coaching position this week.
