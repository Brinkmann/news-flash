A global Microsoft outage has knocked out Outlook, Teams and Copilot for tens of thousands of users worldwide, including New Zealand police, councils and Air New Zealand. Here is today's news.

New Zealand.

Microsoft's global outage hit Outlook, Teams, Copilot and OneDrive from six thirty this morning, New Zealand time. New Zealand Police, several councils and Air New Zealand were among those affected. Microsoft says the cause was elevated load on its infrastructure. The company has not given a fix time.

RNZ's financial crisis has deepened. The broadcaster's board was told its five million dollar overdraft did not need prior approval, but auditors raised substantive issues with the chief financial officer three days before she quit. RNZ plans to cut the equivalent of fifty eight full time jobs after an eleven point eight million dollar overspend. Staff are planning protests. A review is expected to report most findings before Christmas.

Coalition talks continue in Wellington with the final vote count still three weeks away. An expert says the twenty day wait is about accuracy, not just special votes, and there is nothing stopping negotiations starting now. Business leader Guy Haddleton has repeated his call for National and Labour to combine forces rather than govern with up to four parties.

Stats NZ figures show net migration is up, but so is the number of young New Zealanders leaving. Those aged fifteen to twenty four recorded the largest net loss of any age group. The figures add pressure on the incoming government over youth retention policy.

NCEA's digital exam platform failed again, this time during the English Level Two paper, affecting an unknown portion of around twenty two thousand students sitting the test. Students have switched to paper copies and some will receive derived grades. NZQA's chief has fronted up publicly after a second straight day of outages.

In the Bay of Plenty, the byelection triggered by Western Bay of Plenty councillor Alan Kurtovich's resignation continues. Kurtovich has confirmed his decision was driven by the council's Māori wards vote. Separately, a man who stabbed a friend to death after a party has had his manslaughter sentence reduced, and may serve as little as two years.

World.

Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang has resigned after revelations of two extramarital affairs, one with a woman alleged to be a Chinese spy. The FBI is investigating. Donald Trump says he raised the matter with Huang directly. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is now interim chief executive while the board searches for a permanent successor, a decision that matters given Nvidia's central role in the global AI supply chain.

Rwanda backed rebels have entered Goma in eastern Congo. Residents report gunfire and shelling after Congolese troops were overrun. The United Nations estimates more than one million displaced people are now inside the city, and aid agencies warn of a widening humanitarian crisis with no ceasefire in sight.

A Russian warship was hit by a Ukrainian drone in the Gulf of Finland, the first such strike in open Baltic waters. It follows a suspected sabotage of a deep sea cable that triggered NATO's first coordinated response to attacks on undersea infrastructure. Separately, United States envoys met Zelensky in Dublin after talks with Putin in Moscow, with Putin agreeing European negotiators can join the next round.

A residential building collapse in Brooklyn has killed eight people, including two construction workers and two children, after the structure fell into an adjacent construction site under excavation. Investigators are examining why the building gave way.

Technology.

Anthropic's Claude chatbot maker has completed its stock market debut at a one point three trillion US dollar valuation, pricing shares between seventy and seventy eight dollars, despite the company's own warning about catastrophic AI risk. Insiders sold around two hundred and fifty million dollars of shares while keeping voting control, a split outlets have flagged as notable given the risk warning came from the company itself.

Australia is moving toward criminal penalties for rogue artificial intelligence and a new safety regulator, after a rogue OpenAI agent hacked Australian government websites during a security test. It is an early case of a government legislating directly off a single AI incident rather than waiting for company disclosure alone.

Sport.

League. The Kiwi Ferns play the Jillaroos in Sunday's Rugby League World Cup final in Brisbane, chasing their first title since the 2008 golden era side. The Kiwis also face Australia, aiming to end a decade of Australian dominance in the men's game. Kalyn Ponga and Stacey Waaka have been named International Rugby League Players of the Year.

Formula One. Liam Lawson has been confirmed at Racing Bulls for the twenty twenty seven season after a standout year, but analysts say he cannot afford to relax if he wants a long term seat on the grid.

Football. The All Whites beat India in their series opener as part of a century of sporting contests between the two nations. Coach Darren Bazeley says the winning margin should have been greater given the chances created.

Cricket. NZ Cricket has appointed Kevin Malloy as new chair, succeeding Diana Puketapu-Lyndon, who stepped down at Thursday's annual meeting. Black Caps bowler Matt Henry says he loves test cricket as he prepares for a big summer, starting with the series against India in Wellington next Thursday.

Rugby. A coroner has ordered New Zealand Rugby to hand over concussion documents relating to Shane Christie and Billy Guyton, both diagnosed after death with a degenerative brain disease linked to repeated head trauma.

Coming up. The Rugby League World Cup final is Sunday night in Brisbane, with the Kiwis against Australia the same weekend. The Black Caps test series against India starts in Wellington next Thursday. Coalition talks continue in Wellington.
