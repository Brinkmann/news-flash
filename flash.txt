A record loss for Tauranga's former council chief executive has been confirmed, with Marty Grenfell paid more than one hundred and fifty thousand dollars after resigning over the Mount Maunganui landslide review.

New Zealand.

Tauranga City Council has disclosed severance payments to former chief executive Marty Grenfell following his resignation after the Mount Maunganui landslide review. He received a severance payment of one hundred and thirty thousand, seven hundred and ninety two dollars, plus twenty one thousand, five hundred and twenty six dollars towards legal fees, a total of more than one hundred and fifty two thousand dollars. The landslide killed six people after the council ignored successive warnings.

RNZ staff protested in the rain outside the broadcaster's Auckland and Wellington offices for a second time, with a petition launched against plans to cut fifty eight jobs. An eleven point eight million dollar deficit from organisational failures is behind the cuts. David Seymour wants a tougher, faster inquiry into RNZ completed by Christmas, while Labour wants the whole board sacked and replaced. Separately, RNZ is investigating a possible cyber breach, a day after Allied Press was hit by an attack.

KMD Brands, owner of Kathmandu and Rip Curl, has pulled out of takeover talks with Bluestar Alliance. Shares fell more than fourteen per cent to two dollars and six cents on Tuesday. The company's annual meeting heard further cost cuts are needed.

Police are searching farmland and bush near Timaru for a missing seventy two year old man, Steven Pringle, who failed to return from an hour long bike ride in the Mackenzie district yesterday. His e-bike has been found. The search continues today.

Nearly a quarter tonne of drugs has been seized in a coordinated global crackdown on dark web suppliers, with class A and B substances found hidden inside Kiwi onion soup sachets. Police say the sachets were used to try to avoid detection before reaching New Zealand communities.

In the Bay of Plenty, Western Bay of Plenty councillor Alan Kurtovich says his decision to quit over the district's Māori wards decision was, in his words, absolutely the right one, despite triggering a byelection and its cost to ratepayers.

World.

Nvidia shares have fallen seven per cent after the United States attorney general declined to confirm whether the company's chief executive Jensen Huang was under investigation. It follows Huang's reinstatement last week, which had added nearly one trillion US dollars to the company's value. Donald Trump faces backlash after saying he personally convinced Nvidia to bring Huang back, with lawmakers now demanding answers about his involvement.

Huawei has been found guilty of stealing trade secrets in a New York trial, but acquitted on the more serious charge of racketeering conspiracy. The case followed a months long trial into the Chinese telecommunications giant.

NATO's chief has vowed to defend against threats in space, after German media reported Russia is planning to launch a nuclear armed satellite within weeks. The alliance is treating the reports as a serious escalation.

Households in Britain are braced for higher energy bills, with regulator Ofgem due to announce a new price cap that will raise domestic gas prices significantly from January. Millions of households will also receive a one hundred pound payment to help offset the rise.

Technology.

Nvidia's chief executive Jensen Huang has been in therapy after admitting to what he called terrible mistakes, apologising for violating the company's code of conduct. It follows his resignation and reinstatement within the same week, a sequence that added almost one trillion US dollars to Nvidia's share price and lifted global markets. The United States has separately moved to blacklist Singapore based Megaspeed, accused of funnelling AI chips to China after a former executive's affair with Huang became public.

An Anthropic mathematician says he may have used artificial intelligence to help prove the Riemann hypothesis, one of mathematics' most famous unsolved problems. The claim has not yet been independently verified, with the company itself saying checking the work will take time.

Sport.

Netball. Former Silver Ferns captain Julie Carter has died, aged sixty, remembered as competitive, clever and committed. New Zealand netball has now lost two former Silver Fern captains this year.

Real Madrid beat PSV Eindhoven five one, with a stunning goal from Jude Bellingham and two from Kylian Mbappe.
