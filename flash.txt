New Zealand.

Bay of Plenty Police confirm a body has been recovered from the water following a crash on Chapel Street, Tauranga overnight. This follows yesterday's search by police divers for a second occupant after a vehicle entered the water.

Western Bay of Plenty's Māori wards decision triggered a byelection, with former councillor Alan Kurtovich confirming to the Herald that the decision was the reason he quit. He says it was the right call despite the cost of forcing a fresh vote.

Starship Hospital's nursing shortage is now described by staff as the worst they have seen, with cancer treatment delayed for some patients. RNZ reports a lack of experienced nurses able to deliver chemotherapy, and new graduates cannot fill the gap. Health New Zealand has not said how many treatments are affected.

Fuel prices have topped four dollars a litre, with BP's New Lynn station advertising ninety five octane at four eleven on Friday. An AA spokesperson says prices are expected to stay high until at least Christmas, with rising shipping costs on oil cargoes a factor.

On the campaign trail, National has promised fewer council approvals for farms, with Christopher Luxon and agriculture spokesman Todd McClay unveiling the policy at AgFest in Greymouth. The Greens have promised a universal payment for trainee teachers, nurses, vets and dentists, saying students should not have to go broke training for jobs the country needs. ACT leader David Seymour has again raised the possibility of a coalition including the Opportunity Party, something National has repeatedly ruled out.

The Employment Court has ruled on the Calendar Girls case, a decision the Aotearoa Sex Workers Collective says sets an important precedent for the country. Five dancers pursued the case.

World.

Ukraine's Operation Vivaldi has reclaimed two hundred and seventy seven square kilometres of territory in the eastern Donbas region, the BBC reports, though relations with President Trump have soured. Trump has called Ukraine's latest strikes on Russian refineries a disgrace, and President Zelensky says Ukraine has the right to defend itself. European Union leaders have rallied around Zelensky.

Rwanda backed M23 rebels have entered Goma in the Democratic Republic of Congo, with the United Nations estimating more than one million displaced people now inside the city, the Wall Street Journal and BBC report.

White House press secretary Katie Zacharia has been fired after five days in the job. Donald Trump signalled her departure on Friday, a week after appointing her, reported by Stuff, the Herald and Al Jazeera.

At least thirty three pupils and a teacher were killed in Pakistan when a school roof collapsed onto three classrooms, police say.

Technology.

Financial regulators here are warning of rising artificial intelligence enabled cyber attacks. The Reserve Bank and the Financial Markets Authority say regulated industries must prepare, after a Russian man was arrested in Germany over a hack on London hospital blood testing firm Synnovis, reported by RNZ and the BBC.

Anthropic is seeking new investors ahead of a planned stock market debut, the BBC reports, as some investors grow concerned about the scale of spending on artificial intelligence infrastructure. Tech sector leaders here are separately renewing calls for a long term cross party plan supporting growth in digital services, AI and quantum technology.

Sport.

League. Rugby League World Cup. Toa Samoa thrashed France in their opening match. Fetu Samoa lost forty nil to the Jillaroos but won praise from Australia for their performance. The Kiwi Ferns prepare for their own World Cup opener, looking to repeat their dominant 2023 campaign.

Rugby. The All Blacks play the Wallabies in Sydney today in the second Bledisloe Cup test, with Fabian Holland returning among eight changes. All Blacks coach Dave Rennie has criticised the World Cup draw as crazy, warning it could see New Zealand meet the Springboks in the quarter finals. The Black Ferns face France in Hamilton today, Shyrah Tuliau-Tua'a named on the wing.

Netball. The Constellation Cup series against Australia begins this weekend. Coach Dame Noeline Taurua says the pressure is on the Australians, in her first time coaching the Silver Ferns on home soil in two years.

Cycling. Southland's Tom Sexton won silver at the World Track Championships in Shanghai. Great Britain's Mark Stewart and Anna Morris both won gold in the points race and omnium.

Rugby. Northland booked their first ever NPC grand final spot, beating Otago thirty eight to nineteen in Whangārei.

Football. Auckland FC defended their decision to play striker Sam Cosgrove in the A-League season opener despite a prior sending off, saying his ban was already served in National League fixtures.
