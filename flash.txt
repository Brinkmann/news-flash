Inflation has tumbled to three point seven percent, but economists warn the drop will be short lived, with a Reserve Bank rate decision due next week.

New Zealand.

Annual inflation to September came in at three point seven percent, down from the previous quarter. Petrol price falls in July and August drove the dip. Economists say prices are expected to rise again soon, and there are now strong odds the Reserve Bank hikes the Official Cash Rate next week, likely by twenty five basis points. Separately, oil prices have hit their highest level since May, on reports the United States is again considering strikes on Iran, a move that could push fuel costs back up here.

TVNZ staff will strike for the first time in twenty years over pay. The E tū union says the state broadcaster offered workers a two and a half percent rise, well below inflation. No strike date has been confirmed yet.

The election campaign enters its final week before polling day on November the seventh. Video footage has emerged showing a Labour candidate with gang members at a family function. Kerrin Leoni says she has whānau in a gang and attended the event earlier this year. National says the footage confirms what Labour is trying to hide. Separately, enrolment remains below twenty twenty three levels with three days left, and fewer than sixty nine percent of eighteen to twenty four year olds are signed up.

Trains across Auckland faced cancellations and delays this morning after ventilation infrastructure issues closed the City Rail Link tunnel ahead of the morning rush. The East West, South City and Onehunga West lines were all affected. Services have since reopened but disruption continues.

In Tauranga, the Western Bay of Plenty byelection triggered by Alan Kurtovich's resignation over the district's Māori wards decision remains under way, after Kurtovich confirmed the decision was the direct cause of his quitting.

World.

Meta has been fined twenty five billion dollars over what regulators called lies about user data privacy, the largest such penalty against the company to date, larger than its previous eighteen billion dollar settlement in California.

Russian missile strikes hit Ukraine in three waves, hours after Russia said it was ready in principle to resume peace talks with the United States and Ukraine. Doubts remain over whether the strikes signal genuine willingness to negotiate.

Rwanda backed M23 rebels have entered the Congolese city of Goma, with residents reporting ongoing gunfire and shelling. The United Nations estimates more than one million displaced people are now inside the city.

At least four people have died after storms brought flooding to southern France, including two women found drowned in a cellar in the Bouches du Rhône region. Two new storms, Austen and Boelo, are also forecast to bring further stormy weather to the United Kingdom this weekend.

Technology.

A critical security flaw in PHP, a programming language used across much of the internet, triggered a global scramble this week after researchers said artificial intelligence helped uncover vulnerabilities described as among the most significant ever found. Websites were taken offline worldwide as companies raced to patch systems. The UK government says five hundred new vulnerabilities have been found in its own systems as a result. Separately, Barclays says it is cutting five thousand jobs, a five percent reduction in its workforce, as it increases its reliance on artificial intelligence.

Sport.

Glenn Phillips top scored for New Zealand. Captain Mitchell Santner says the side's preparation gives him confidence ahead of the rest of the tour. Separately, rain washed out the White Ferns' chance of a win in the Rose Bowl series, with Australia already retaining the trophy after winning the first two games.

Cricket, England. Sri Lanka pulled off the second biggest run chase in One Day International history, overturning Harry Brook's two hundred, the fastest double century in the format, to win with a six off the final ball.

Netball. The Silver Ferns' win over Australia in Christchurch has put them in a commanding position to win the Constellation Cup for just the fourth time. They travel to Australia for the final two tests.
