A watchdog has cleared Police Commissioner Richard Chambers of workplace conduct concerns, as three buses collided outside Auckland's Botany Town Centre injuring three people.

New Zealand.

Police Commissioner Richard Chambers has been cleared by the Independent Police Conduct Authority, which found no evidence to substantiate workplace conduct concerns raised against him. It was the watchdog's second investigation into Chambers. Separately, the same watchdog backed an officer who fired fourteen shots at a wanted man's car as it reversed towards him near Rotorua, finding he genuinely believed he faced death or serious harm.

Three buses collided outside Botany Town Centre in East Tamaki yesterday. At least three people were hurt, two taken to Middlemore Hospital in moderate condition. Chapel Road was closed northbound while emergency services responded.

Police are investigating businessman Harry Mowbray over his Split Your Vote campaign, which urges National supporters to give their electorate vote to National and party vote to ACT or New Zealand First. National has pushed back against the strategy. The investigation comes in the campaign's final week before Saturday's election.

More than nine hundred thousand people have already voted, a quarter of enrolled voters, compared with about seven hundred thousand at the same point in 2023. A new Ipsos poll extends Labour's lead over National on the top five voter concerns. Labour has promised to stop phone, internet and insurance companies charging loyal customers more than new ones.

Police are searching for Mary Coakley, sixty three, missing from Invercargill since yesterday. She has dementia and is considered vulnerable. Her family say her disappearance is not normal and they want her home.

Revolut has become New Zealand's twenty seventh bank after gaining a Reserve Bank registration, the country's first digital-only bank. It will offer savings accounts and credit cards, and has been rated triple-B-plus by Standard and Poor's with a positive outlook.

World.

Russian bombing killed twenty six people at a Ukrainian aid centre, Kyiv says. Officials say at least nine three thousand kilogram aerial bombs hit a southern village. President Zelensky called it utterly horrific. Fighting has also returned to the Kherson region after the longest ceasefire of the war, a six day truce that has not altered the wider conflict.

A cyber attack hit shop freezers across seven European countries, pushing temperatures as high as fifty degrees Celsius. Supermarkets dumped huge quantities of spoiled food. The incident has raised questions about the vulnerability of other connected retail technology.

Americans vote in midterm elections tomorrow, New Zealand time. A Reuters-Ipsos poll puts President Trump's approval near a record low, at thirty two percent. Control of the Senate and House is at stake, in what BBC Verify says is the most expensive midterm campaign on record, projected to cost close to twelve billion US dollars.

A Learjet crash in South Florida killed nine people, including a technology entrepreneur, his family and their nanny. The co-pilot has been identified in reports as twenty years old. Investigators are examining air traffic audio in which a crew member can be heard shouting a warning before impact.

Technology.

Google's parent company Alphabet has lost about five hundred billion US dollars in market value after revealing its own artificial intelligence search summaries are cutting into advertising revenue. The stock hit a six month low. It is part of a wider sell-off, with the Nasdaq at its lowest level in more than two months, as investors question whether nearly one trillion US dollars in artificial intelligence spending is matched by returns. Intel shares have also continued falling.

Newsroom reports Christopher Luxon used a non-government artificial intelligence account for official Prime Ministerial business, which the Cabinet Manual discourages for personal email. The matter has not been addressed by Luxon's office.

Sport.

Rugby League World Cup. The Kiwis have lost forward Jacob Laban for their semi-final against Tonga. He is ruled out with injury and will miss the rest of the tournament. Nine people were arrested in South Auckland after Rugby League World Cup celebrations following Tonga's twenty to eighteen win over Samoa, which already aired.

All Blacks. The team's head of security for a decade, Logan Cadwallader, has died in Edinburgh after a medical event ahead of Saturday's test against Scotland at Murrayfield. Assistant coach Jason Ryan paid an emotional tribute, saying the squad is heartbroken but will be ready to go.

Formula One. Liam Lawson has moved up two places after post-race penalties at the Mexican Grand Prix, crossing the line ninth and gaining positions on review. It follows his disqualification from qualifying earlier in the weekend.

Football. Uncapped Wellington Phoenix players feature in Darren Bazeley's final All Whites squad, more than half of whom first face each other in the season's first New Zealand derby in Auckland on Saturday, before linking up for a match against India.

Netball. Netball New Zealand says it has an advanced plan for the 2027 domestic competition, after the collapse of a privatised league deal with Anna Mowbray and Ali Williams. The Waikato Bay of Plenty Magic says the model would have been out of reach for at least one existing franchise, and players face months of uncertainty over their professional futures.

Coming up. The Kiwis play Tonga and the Kiwi Ferns play Samoa in Rugby League World Cup semi-finals this week. The All Blacks play Scotland at Murrayfield on Saturday. Election day is Saturday the seventh of November.

End of flash.
