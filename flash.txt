Firefighters evacuated a seven storey Auckland building floor by floor after an alarm failure, and the High Court has ruled the government breached women's rights by scrapping pay equity claims.

New Zealand.

Firefighters were sent to every floor of the Clearpoint building in central Auckland after its alarm system failed to activate properly. Crews evacuated occupants manually, and streets nearby were closed with cars trapped in the basement. Staff in surrounding workplaces were told to stay inside while crews worked through the building.

The High Court has ruled the coalition government's changes to the Equal Pay Act discriminated against women and were inconsistent with the Bill of Rights Act. The changes had scrapped dozens of pay equity claims and made it harder to lodge new ones. Workers have welcomed the ruling. Parliament must now deal with pay equity on two fronts, according to a former National MP.

Fonterra has raised its forecast farmgate milk price, narrowing the range to between nine dollars sixty and ten dollars sixty per kilogram of milk solids, citing strong Chinese demand. West Coast dairy farmers are on track for a record five hundred million dollar payout, with dairy making up more than thirteen percent of the region's economy.

A documentary crew was allowed into fugitive Tom Phillips' camp before forensic examination work was finished, and police have admitted officers who objected to the film crew's presence were overruled. Police say they failed to treat mainstream media fairly, breaching best practice during the four year hunt.

Wayne Brown has drawn further criticism after footage emerged of him praising a book of Xi Jinping's speeches on Chinese state television, calling Xi a very important man. Labour has distanced itself from New Zealand First MP Willie Jackson's comments that the United States ambassador should apologise or be sent packing over remarks about Brown. A law professor has called the ambassador's criticism of Brown undiplomatic.

In the Bay of Plenty, a man has had his sentence reduced for stabbing a friend to death after a party and may now serve as little as two years for manslaughter. Western Bay of Plenty's byelection, triggered by councillor Alan Kurtovich's resignation over the Māori wards decision, remains in progress.

World.

Trump, Putin and Xi have issued a joint peace declaration after their first trilateral meeting, held in Shenzhen. Analysts say the summit was high on spectacle and short on substance. European leaders meeting in Berlin say they have a united negotiating position for talks with Russia on ending the war in Ukraine. Putin has separately vowed a far more painful response after Ukraine's strikes on the Kerch Bridge.

The International Court of Justice has ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya minority, in an unprecedented verdict concerning a military crackdown that killed thousands in 2017. Rohingya survivors in Bangladesh have called for perpetrators to face consequences. Lawyers will now study how Gambia proved Myanmar intended to commit genocide, a precedent with implications beyond this case.

Israel's military has completed a ground operation in Gaza beyond the agreed Yellow Line, which it says dismantled a tunnel network. Hamas has called the incursion a flagrant ceasefire violation. Freed Israeli hostages released under the ceasefire deal were found still carrying shrapnel from the October seventh attack, with some held alone in tunnels for up to eight months.

Technology.

Anthropic has completed a record breaking stock market debut, raising eighty one billion US dollars and becoming one of the world's most valuable public companies. It is the first purely artificial intelligence company to list publicly. Seven Anthropic founders are now multi billionaires. Three protesters were arrested outside Nasdaq during the float. Analysts question whether the listing can ease fears of a wider AI investment bubble.

A security flaw has been found in Java, the world's third largest programming language, discovered by the same company that previously found a critical flaw in PHP. Artificial intelligence was used to uncover the bug. The disclosure adds to a recent run of technology outages, though officials say there is no evidence the incidents are linked.

Sport.

Rugby. Beauden Barrett will play his hundred and fiftieth test when the All Blacks face England at Twickenham on Saturday at three ten a.m. New Zealand time, with changes across the run-on side. England are boosted by the return of George Furbank, Seb Atkinson and Ollie Chessum. Attack coach Mike Blair says the response to the Wales loss will be focused rather than angry.

Cricket. Henry Nicholls scored a century to resurrect the Black Caps on day one against India at the Basin Reserve, after early bowling damage. Glenn Phillips also reached a century before falling on day two. High profile Black Caps including Trent Boult, Matt Henry and Rachin Ravindra have been released by their Indian Premier League franchises.

Mixed martial arts. Navajo Stirling could set up an all New Zealand light heavyweight title fight against teammate Carlos Ulberg, if he beats Jiří Procházka this weekend.

Basketball. The New Zealand men's team has taken an unassailable two nil series lead over the Silver Ferns with a dominant sixty four to forty six win in Hamilton.

Football. Auckland FC coach Pedro Moreira says watching the All Whites train at the club's home ground gave him fresh ideas as his side look to revive their A-League campaign.
