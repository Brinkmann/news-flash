Michael Laws faces an uncertain future in New Zealand First after sexist comments about a National candidate, as the election campaign reaches its final days.

New Zealand.

Winston Peters says Michael Laws' future with New Zealand First is still to be decided, after Laws made sexist remarks about National's Katie Nimon. Christopher Luxon says Laws will never be a minister in his government. Peters called the ongoing campaign the worst he has seen. Election day is Saturday. Christopher Luxon has taken his campaign bus from the Hutt Valley toward Auckland, urging supporters to stick to the plan in the final push.

Unemployment has risen to five point seven percent, worse than expected, raising the risk of what economists call labour scarring, where people who stay out of work too long find it harder to return. Teenagers on Jobseeker support fear homelessness after their parents' income was brought into the benefit test. House prices have recorded a seventh consecutive monthly decline, now flat in real terms over a decade. Global Dairy Trade prices rose a further three percent overnight, a second straight rise, following a two and a half percent increase at the previous auction.

Firefighters' union members are threatening ten hour strikes, with Fire and Emergency warning of extreme risk to the public if they proceed. The union wants the government to intervene in stalled bargaining with its employer. Separately, Fire and Emergency says it has already dealt with a spate of fires caused by stray fireworks ahead of Guy Fawkes night.

In Western Bay of Plenty, councillor Alan Kurtovich has confirmed his resignation was triggered by the council's Māori wards decision, forcing a byelection. Dallas Hohua, who stabbed a friend to death after a party, has had his manslaughter sentence reduced and may serve as little as two years.

One person has died and four were badly hurt in a crash at an intersection near Westmere, Whanganui, overnight Tuesday. Two people remain in critical condition.

World.

Democrats have taken control of both chambers of the United States Congress after sweeping midterm election wins, with Hakeem Jeffries promising a focus on affordability. President Trump has acknowledged Republicans' heavy losses, partly blaming the conflict with Iran. Elon Musk spent close to five hundred million US dollars backing Republican candidates without delivering the results he wanted. Republicans are now turning on each other over the scale of the defeat.

Russian strikes on a village in Zaporizhzhia have killed at least forty people, with Ukraine warning another thirty may still be trapped under rubble from Monday's bombing. Bodycam footage reviewed by Ukrainian officials appears to show a double tap strike, where a second missile hit after rescuers arrived.

Binance says fake job interviews were used to target an engineer in the six point three billion US dollar crypto hack blamed on North Korean hackers, which North Korea denies. Users have withdrawn three point six billion US dollars from the exchange since the breach. A separate cyber attack hit supermarket freezers across several European countries, pushing temperatures as high as fifty degrees and spoiling stock in countries including Ireland, Sweden and Belgium.

Technology.

Google's market value has fallen by about five hundred billion US dollars after the company revealed its own artificial intelligence search summaries are cutting into advertising revenue. The stock hit a six month low. It comes as the Nasdaq sits at its lowest level in more than two months, with investors questioning whether almost one trillion US dollars in artificial intelligence spending is paying off. Intel shares have continued to fall. Google has also announced it is turning off artificial intelligence tools for schoolchildren by default worldwide, starting in the United States, with no date yet set for rollout.

Sport.

Rugby league. The Kiwis play Tonga and the Kiwi Ferns play Samoa in Rugby League World Cup semifinals this week, with Ponga back in the Kiwis side. Tonga's twenty to eighteen win over Samoa has renewed talk of an annual Pacific rivalry series.

All Blacks. The team prepares to face Scotland at Murrayfield on Saturday, New Zealand time Sunday morning. Attack coach Mike Blair, a former Scotland international now with the All Blacks, says he is fully aware of the challenge his new side faces against a Scotland team chasing a first win over New Zealand.

Cricket. India thrashed the Black Caps in the first of five one day internationals, with Virat Kohli among the runs. Devon Conway remains out after a head clash with Matt Henry at training. Coach Rob Walter is managing a squad already stretched thin by injuries.

Golf. LIV Golf is set to wind down after its financing backer withdrew support, ending the breakaway tour's run.

Tennis. Novak Djokovic is out of contention for the ATP Finals in Turin after a second round loss to qualifier Rinky Hijikata at the Paris Masters.

Netball. Ali Williams has ruled out a rebel league after his proposed privatised competition deal with Netball New Zealand collapsed.

Youth Olympics. New Zealand swimmer Ariel Muchirahondo has won a second gold, adding the one hundred metres backstroke to his two hundred metres title.
